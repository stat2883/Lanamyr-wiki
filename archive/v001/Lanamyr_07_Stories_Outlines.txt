STORIES AND LEGENDS — STORY CONCEPTS
================================================================================

--- UNTITLED — THE SHADOW BEFORE THE STORM ---
ERA: 2nd Epoch, during the lead-up to and early years of the Great War.
CONCEPT: An unnamed group of adventurers navigating Lanamyr as the world begins
to darken around them. Creatures of Olum appearing in places they shouldn't,
trade routes going quiet, people disappearing. Adomorn and Karma may appear.
Ground-level view of the Great War era.
STATUS: Concept only. Characters, specific plot, and scope entirely undeveloped.

--- UNLIT (WORKING TITLE) — THE PARALLEL ---
ERA: 3rd Epoch, 366-392 AW.
WORKING TITLE: Unlit. Other titles considered: Two Flames, The Same War, Not
Yet, Before the Collision. Title not yet settled.

CONCEPT: A parallel story following Mya Li and Diquyk Lockwood — from their
youth through the full course of the Elf War (387-392 AW), culminating in the
final battle inside the Dark Elf kingdom and Diquyk's banishment. Told in
alternating chapter blocks — not strictly one chapter per character, but groups
of chapters before switching POV.

READER APPROACH: The reader is assumed to have no prior knowledge of Lanamyr.
The world is revealed entirely through the characters' lived experience.
Show the world, never explain it.

NARRATIVE STRUCTURE:
Diquyk and Mya each narrate their own chapters in first or close-third person.
The chapter blocks are not strictly alternating — several consecutive chapters
may follow one character before switching to the other. In the early portions
of the story, Diquyk's arc requires more runway (spanning ~26 years from
age 14 through the Elf War) while Mya's arc spans approximately 5 years from
joining the militia. The grouped block structure allows each character's
momentum to build before switching rather than constantly interrupting.
As the two timelines converge toward the final battle, the blocks may naturally
shorten and shift more frequently, building pace.

The two chapters covering the direct encounters between Mya and Diquyk use an
unnamed omniscient external narrator — a purely literary device signaling that
something has shifted beyond either character's individual perspective.
No frame narrator for the wider story. The Galstaff-as-narrator idea was
considered and shelved — not discarded.

ENDING: Confirmed. The story ends from Mya's POV. Diquyk's banishment is a
threshold rather than a conclusion — the start of a new chapter for him, not
the end of the book. Mya's perspective earns the ending: she is left standing
in The Deep with everything she came with gone — her soldiers, Kharis already
weeks behind her, Adomorn apparently dead beside her. The victory is real but
the cost is total. The reader knows more than she does, which makes it land
harder.

TRANSITIONAL IMAGE (confirmed):
In one of Mya's chapters near the convergence point, the chapter ends with Mya
and her forces advancing toward battle and seeing a large blue flame in the
distance. The chapter closes on that image — wordlessly, without commentary.
The next chapter opens with the omniscient external narrator. This is the hinge
point between Mya's individual POV and the shared external perspective of their
first major encounter. The less Mya says about the flame, the more powerful
the landing.

THREE-ACT SHAPE:
Act One: Both characters' early years. Diquyk from 366 AW (age 14, the
  incident) through his corruption in 383 AW and his recruitment of the Stone
  Giants and Dark Elves in 383-387 AW. Mya from her restless scholarly years
  in Caldalus through joining the militia in 387 AW.
Act Two: The Elf War years. The two-front assault builds to the first large
  encounter as its midpoint crisis — Mya's most crushing defeat. Kharis killed.
Act Three: Reformation and convergence. Mya rebuilds, forms the Marauders,
  Adomorn enters the picture. The final battle and Diquyk's banishment.

CHAPTER OUTLINES — DIQUYK'S ARC:

DQ-1: THE INCIDENT (366 AW)
  DQ wakes with an uneasy feeling — something different in his body, not named,
  not understood. He can sense the ambient energy field around him — closer and
  more present than usual. He reaches toward it instinctively, without
  understanding what it is or what he is doing.
  STREET ENCOUNTER: Before the incident, DQ and his parents are out in town and
  briefly bump into Degeric. A casual, ordinary exchange. DQ's internal
  observations during the encounter do the character work — the way his parents
  respond to Degeric, DQ's own read of someone he has known his whole life.
  Short, specific, ordinary. The reader builds Degeric from what DQ notices.
  THE INCIDENT: The power does not come out once. It erupts in repeated bursts —
  brilliant white-blue flashes of light. The first burst scatters bystanders and
  draws guards toward the disturbance. DQ's perspective stays throughout: fear
  and confusion, not rage or intention. This is happening to him. Between bursts
  he sees what the previous burst did. White-blue flash — blink — the world has
  changed. The charred remains of his parents discovered in intervals between
  flashes he cannot stop. The guards drawn by the first burst die for the
  instinct that made them approach. Horror through helplessness.
  NOTE: The white-blue color of these bursts is the same blue flame that defines
  DQ's magic throughout his entire life — present from the very first expression
  of his power, continuous with the blue flame Mya sees in the distance before
  the first encounter.
  The chapter ends when Degeric arrives — a familiar face in the aftermath. DQ
  recognizes him. Degeric steps toward him rather than away. The chapter closes
  on that approach. The conversation belongs to DQ-2.

DQ-2: THE YEARS WITH DEGERIC — EARLY (366–~370 AW)
  Degeric helps DQ process his grief in the immediate aftermath. Takes him along
  on his church duties — DQ witnesses Degeric help the less fortunate, protect
  the vulnerable, earn the respect of those around him. A father figure without
  being overbearing — Degeric understands he cannot replace what was lost.
  DQ is an eager student of both magic and the gods. Degeric offers rudimentary
  explanation of the ambient energy. DQ learns quickly and begins to exceed what
  Degeric can teach. DQ also experiments beyond what Degeric shows him.
  OLUM CONTEXT: As a young child before the incident, DQ would have heard of or
  witnessed Olum attacks from inside his home, locked away while town guards
  handled the creatures. After the incident, with Degeric, he crosses to the
  other side of that door. His first Olum encounter as a participant happens
  within the first year to eighteen months. Degeric instructs him to keep a safe
  distance and use small spells from afar while Degeric and the town guards
  handle the most dangerous creatures. Subsequent Olum encounters are referenced
  more briefly, showing growing power and confidence.
  THE FLAME INTERCEPTION SCENE (confirmed): A scene in which Degeric attempts to
  produce a small magical flame in his palm and DQ intercepts the ambient energy
  before it reaches him. From DQ's perspective, the sensory experience is like
  standing in a pool and feeling the current flow past him toward Degeric —
  and simply redirecting it. Degeric's flame fails. They both feel the shift.
  The dynamic between them changes permanently in that moment — the teacher has
  reached his ceiling. Specific prose details to be decided at writing stage.
  The chapter closes on this scene.

DQ-3: THE YEARS WITH DEGERIC — LATE (~370–376 AW)
  Opens in the new dynamic — Degeric now mentor rather than teacher. Urging DQ
  to use his power to help people, to be a protector. DQ takes that to heart
  and begins doing exactly that within the church and the town.
  Subsequent Olum encounters are handled briefly showing DQ's continued growth
  and increasing capability.
  NEAR THE END OF THE CHAPTER: Degeric receives word of a newly formed Olum nest
  in the region. He and DQ take a couple of the town's guards with them to raid
  it. The nest raid is the emotional climax of the chapter. During the raid,
  Degeric and the guards fall into grave danger. Degeric pleads with DQ to
  unleash his full power. DQ hesitates — his mind flashes back to the incident
  and his parents. After a few moments of internal struggle, he gives in to the
  power within him and kills all remaining Olum in a series of directed magical
  bursts of blue flame — controlled enough that Degeric and the guards are
  unharmed. This is the first time DQ reaches for the full extent of his power
  deliberately and under real stakes. He finds out in the worst possible moment
  that he is not the uncontrolled thing that killed his parents. He can choose.
  Degeric directly addresses the nest event with DQ afterward.
  The nest raid catalyzes the departure conversation. Degeric points DQ toward
  a direction — a region of rumored Olum activity, framed as nest hunting, likely
  through his church network or reports from travelers. TENTATIVE — specific
  direction undeveloped. The departure conversation closes the chapter. The
  parting is warm and clean. A loving goodbye neither of them knows is nearly
  the last.

DQ-4: THE GATE YEARS (376–382 AW)
  DQ leaves with a direction Degeric suggested. The chapter lives in the space
  between his old life and the corruption coming — DQ alone, building something
  extraordinary, discovering what he is capable of. Traveling snippets as he
  moves through the world. Gate building. The emotional texture of this chapter
  and specific events within it are largely undeveloped.
  Final visit to Degeric somewhere in 379-381 AW — still pre-corruption.
  Degeric dies 381 AW. Whether DQ was present at the death itself or heard of
  it afterward: undeveloped. How much the final visit echoes the departure
  conversation in DQ-3: worth holding in mind as DQ-4 develops.
  TENTATIVE: DQ's direction when he leaves is toward a region of rumored Olum
  activity suggested by Degeric — framing him as a nest hunter. Details of the
  specific region and events during these years are undeveloped.

DQ-5: THE BURNT PLAINS (383 AW)
  DQ is drawn to the Burnt Plains — an unnatural dead patch in fertile Kimora,
  receiving normal rainfall, supporting nothing. His curiosity is his own; he
  does not know he is being guided. The Abaculus senses his power and draws him
  to the hidden burrow in the battlefield debris, into the chamber below.
  The corruption scene is confirmed in full detail — see the Overview_Cosmology
  file's Abaculus entry, and the confirmed scene excerpt below.
  This is the hinge of DQ's entire arc: the last chapter where he is entirely
  himself. The chapter needs the emotional approach to the plains, and the
  first moments after he surfaces — the beginning of what he now is.

DQ-6: THE STONE GIANTS (383–385 AW)
  DQ travels to the Tempest Peaks. Demonstrates his power as a show of force.
  Offers plunder and the riches of Faelyn. The Giants respect strength; they
  are persuaded. DQ promises to return with additional forces.
  OPEN: What does he show them? What does it feel like for him — performance or
  genuine pleasure? How visible is the flicker of his true self here, if at all?

DQ-7: THE DARK ELVES (385–387 AW)
  DQ travels to The Deep. A different approach from the Giants — the Dark Elves
  chosen for obscurity, deniability, absence of alliances. Equipped with magical
  and enchanted weapons. Gates placed near both locations.
  OPEN: How does the Dark Elf negotiation contrast with the Giants? Colder, more
  strategic? What is DQ's read of them as tools?

DQ-8: THE EARLY WAR YEARS (387–391 AW)
  The war is underway. DQ directs via Jaunt Gates — not on the front lines.
  Four years of reports, adjustments, waiting.
  OPEN: This is the most undeveloped stretch of DQ's arc and one of the most
  important for his inner life. What does he experience during these years?

DQ-9: ESCALATION (391–392 AW)
  DQ decides to make his first major push. What drives the timing? Closes as
  his forces advance into Faelyn in force. Leads directly into Intersection 1.

CHAPTER OUTLINES — MYA'S ARC:

Mya-1: THE SCHOLARLY LIFE (pre-387 AW)
  Opens with Mya waking. Her mind goes over the day ahead — including recent
  trade meetings between Aer'Raenal and Hellar emissaries about trade routes,
  which both she and Phelloden have been sitting in on. The meetings are the
  kind of thing a future queen should find fascinating. Mya does not.
  After getting dressed, Mya meets Phelloden in the library where they have been
  instructed to study the history of Faelyn and neighboring kingdoms. Phelloden
  is engaged — studying maps and researching treaties. Mya has drifted from her
  assigned material to accounts of the Great War, specifically the heroes who
  fought in it. Her mind wanders to wanting to be like them. She stares out a
  window, an open book in front of her.
  A page boy arrives to fetch them at the king's request. Phelloden is immediately
  ready and waiting by the door with the page. Mya has to be called back twice
  from her daydream. From her POV: she registers the scene already assembled
  and waiting — her brother at the door, the page beside him, a slight patience
  in Phelloden's expression. She sighs, returns the book to the shelf, and walks
  slowly toward the door. The contrast in body language between the siblings is
  marked. Mya and Phelloden have a warm relationship — mutual respect, no rivalry,
  genuine humor and teasing between them that emerges naturally. Specific
  moments of humor to arise spontaneously at writing stage.
  OPEN: What specifically does Mya's mind wander to? This is the reader's first
  window into who she is.

Mya-2: THE CONVERSATION WITH PHELLODEN (pre-387 AW)
  Early in the chapter, Mya passes militia members doing training exercises. She
  watches from a distance, partially obscured — she doesn't want to interrupt or
  change what she's seeing. Being the king's daughter, her presence would shift
  the dynamic immediately. She watches their camaraderie, the ease between people
  who've been through something together, their shared laughter. After a few quiet
  moments of observation she moves on, unnoticed.
  The accumulation of small moments — the trade meetings, the library, the militia
  scene — is the real cause of what is coming. Not one single dramatic trigger.
  TENTATIVE: Near the end of the chapter, word reaches the king of a Stone Giant
  attack on the outskirts of Faelyn. This is the final tipping point.
  Mya pulls Phelloden aside. They have their conversation. She steps aside from
  the throne — deliberately, clearly, as her own choice. It is not circumstance.
  Mya then speaks with Aer'Raenal. Not to ask permission — she is decided. She
  convinces him to be okay with it. He tells her who to speak to in the militia.
  Mya speaks with the militia leader. There is a conversation about her status
  as the king's daughter. She is insistent on following the same path as any
  other recruit.
  OPEN: Is the Phelloden conversation one specific exchange or the tipping point
  of a long accumulation? What is said? What does each of them feel?

Mya-3: THE MILITIA (387 AW)
  Mya joins the militia at age 40. The early war has begun. First weeks and
  months — training, the beginning of her relationship with Kharis Ailwin.
  Kharis joins at essentially the same time as Mya. He was among the survivors
  of a Stone Giant attack on a small Wood Elf village who fled to Caldalus to
  deliver word of the attack. His family was killed. That loss is his primary
  motivation for joining — it is raw and immediate in a way Mya's motivation is
  not. Both showed great promise and skill from the start. They spend many hours
  training and talking together. Kharis's story comes out relatively quickly
  given the time and proximity. Mya becomes aware of the asymmetry between their
  reasons for being here. At some point Kharis learns who she is.
  OPEN: Kharis's personality — how they first notice each other. The texture of
  their early bond.

Mya-4: THE EARLY WAR (387–391 AW)
  Four years of two-front pressure. Mya building as a soldier and leader.
  The relationship with Kharis deepens — serious but private. The war is real
  but manageable at this stage. Neither of them knows what is coming.
  OPEN: Specific missions or encounters that shape Mya. The texture of the
  Kharis relationship. What does Faelyn look like under this pressure?

Mya-5: THE BLUE FLAME (late 391/early 392 AW)
  Mya and her forces advance toward the first major engagement with DQ directly.
  The chapter ends with her looking at a large blue flame in the distance.
  No commentary. No interpretation. Just the flame. The chapter closes on that.

THE TWO INTERSECTIONS (both narrated by unnamed omniscient external narrator):

INTERSECTION 1 — THE FIRST ENCOUNTER (392 AW):
  DQ makes his first major push. Dark Elves and Giants under his command carry
  magical and enchanted weapons. He is present on the field for the first time.
  The omniscient narrator holds both of them in frame simultaneously — the only
  voice that can. DQ kills a multitude of soldiers before the Wood Elves retreat.

  The transitional blue flame image finds its explanation here — the reader now
  understands what Mya was looking at when her chapter ended.
  OPEN: Does DQ notice Mya specifically in this encounter?

INTERSECTION 2 — THE FINAL BATTLE (392 AW):
  The Marauders enter The Deep. 8 with Mya as decoy, 2 with Adomorn through the
  gate. DQ senses the transit — the ambush is foiled before it begins. Despite
  this, DQ is engaged on two fronts. He intercepts Adomorn's spells and redirects

  Bow of the Dales. When Mya and Adomorn switch to physical combat, the fuel
  supply is cut. DQ is finally banished. All ten Marauders are dead.
  The omniscient narrator holds the full cost of this without flinching.

ELF WAR — CONFIRMED LORE FOR UNLIT:
STRATEGIC MOTIVATION: The Abaculus directed Diquyk to wage war on Faelyn
  because it had learned of Heaven's Oak through Greyhawk. Breaking the
  Guardians and compromising the chamber was a strategic priority.
TWO-FRONT STRUCTURE: Stone Giants from the Tempest Peaks pressed south into
  Faelyn from the north. Dark Elves from The Deep pressed from the east. Mixed
  units — platoons of Dark Elves with a handful of Giants — formed the front
  lines during the early probing years (387-391 AW).
DIQUYK'S ROLE IN THE EARLY WAR: Directed operations via Jaunt Gates. Not on
  the front lines during 387-391 AW. His personal appearance in 392 AW signaled
  decisive escalation.
RECRUITMENT: Stone Giants first — show of force, promise of plunder. Dark Elves
  second — obscurity, deniability, no alliances, equipped with enchanted weapons.
FINAL BATTLE STRUCTURE: 10 Marauders — 8 with Mya as decoy, 2 with Adomorn
  through the gate. Diquyk sensed the gate transit. All 10 Marauders killed.
ADOMORN'S INTELLIGENCE GAP: Suspected Magni, did not anticipate full power.
  Diquyk possibly the most powerful Magni ever, eclipsing Arias Graymist.

DIQUYK'S CORRUPTION AT THE BURNT PLAINS (confirmed scene, 383 AW):
Diquyk stood before the large crystal. He noted its black color, almost that
of obsidian, though faint swirls seemed to dance across its surface. Drawn to
it by a temptation from the deep recesses of his mind, his hand slowly reached
out. His vision faded and narrowed — first his peripheral vision fell off until
all he could see was the crystal. As he inched forward, the swirls became more
pronounced: a dance of soft greens, blues, and reds in hypnotic patterns. All
thought faded. At the moment of contact, his body immediately tensed, muscles
locking him into a single rigid object. His head was pulled back and for a
brief moment he saw the ceiling of the chamber — but then the swirls, dancing
across the carved stone above him. Diquyk felt his mind collapsing as a strange
foreign presence invaded. He tried to close his eyes and pull his hand away,
but his body would not respond. He tried to shield his mind, escaped into
memories, tried to turn away. The more he struggled, the more dark tendrils
permeated his thoughts. His memories faded one by one until all that was left
was the void. He had nowhere else to go, nothing left to do but submit. In
Diquyk's mind, this struggle seemed to last a while. To an outside observer,
it was just a couple of seconds.

--- UNTITLED — THE RELUCTANT KING ---
ERA: 3rd Epoch, 387-413 AW.
CONCEPT: The story of Gralvin Sutton from his earliest years to the overthrow
of Tarik the Ravager. Beginning with the destruction of his village at age 7,
through 14 years in the arena, to his escape alongside Balric Silverfox, his
discovery of Valeria and her network, the years of quietly helping escaped
slaves and gathering worse and worse reports, and finally the arrival of Mya Li
and the coordinated overthrow that cost him the person he loved most. The story
ends where the legend begins — Gralvin, age 26, being handed a crown he never
wanted.
KEY UNRESOLVED QUESTIONS: The specific circumstances that drew Gralvin and
Balric together. The names and faces of people Gralvin fought in the arena.
The texture of Valeria's village and the world after escape. The full shape of
the coordinated overthrow. The final battle in which Valeria died.
STATUS: Concept only. Broad shape confirmed. Specific scenes, supporting
characters, and emotional detail entirely undeveloped.
