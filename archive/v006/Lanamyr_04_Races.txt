THE DRASU AND DRAAK (1ST EPOCH)
================================================================================

DRASU
Humanoid, larger than any present-day race — tall, bronze-skinned, lean and
powerful. Superior intelligence and magic users. Created the Drasian Empire on
Edura and the Drasian Vault.
THE CARETAKER MANDATE (confirmed): the Drasu were meant to be the caretakers of
Lanamyr in the absence of the Irridae. This is why they were made with
intelligence and greater power at such cost — they were not merely the finest
thing the N'du built, they were built to tend the world once its architects had
gone. Their affinity for the natural world and their impulse to protect it
follow from that purpose rather than from temperament.
WHAT BECAME OF THE MANDATE AT THE SUNDERING: like every other Drasu attribute,
it passed to the descendant races in diminished and partial form — and, like
every other attribute, it did not pass evenly. No single descendant race
carries the whole of it. It fractured into pieces, and separate races hold
separate parts of what was once one role.
CONFIRMED FRAGMENTS:
  - WOOD ELVES — stewardship of forest and growing things. Expressed as
    minimal-impact building and replanting at the forest margins.
  - SHEPHERDS (human subtype) — affinity with animals, at the scale of the
    INDIVIDUAL. A bond with a particular creature, mutually chosen. Expressed
    as partnership, and as a vegetarianism that does not feel like a choice.
  - WATER WHISPERERS (human subtype) — affinity with sea life, at the scale of
    the WHOLE. The sea read as a living system rather than a set of creatures.
    Expressed as restrained and informed harvest rather than abstention.
Each fragment produces a DIFFERENT relationship with the living thing in
question. The Shepherds stopped eating animals; the Wood Elves fell timber and
replant; the Water Whisperers fish and manage the grounds. Tending does not
mean refraining, and the fragments should not be written as variations on one
temperament.
Note that fragments descend by RACE, not by family of races. Two of the four
human subtypes carry one and two do not — Hillfolk and Northmen received no
fragment at all. This is consistent with how every other attribute was
distributed at the Sundering.
FRAGMENTS ARE EXCLUSIVE. This is what separates them from the universal
inheritances such as magic, ingenuity, forging or agriculture, which every race
holds to some degree with the named race merely strongest. A race that does not
hold a given fragment holds NONE of it. There is no faint Hillfolk version of
the Shepherd affinity and no weaker dwarven echo of Wood Elf stewardship. See
TWO CLASSES OF INHERITANCE in the Race Trait Framework below.
NO RACE KNOWS THIS. Each carries a piece of a purpose it cannot name, and none
has any way of recognizing another as holding a matching one.
Adomorn, as the last surviving Drasu, is the last being to hold the mandate
entire, and his 2,500-year vigil over the Abaculus is that function still being
discharged.
OPEN: whether further fragments exist in other races. See Open_Questions. The potential for both good and evil existed within
the Drasu — the Sundering revealed and amplified rather than created these
qualities. Split into elves, dwarves, gnomes, humans, orcs, and others.
Adomorn is the last surviving Drasu. Takes human form. Galstaff Quint is one
of the rare few to have seen his true Drasu form.
THE DRASIAN TONGUE: The Drasu had a language of their own, and it was silenced
at the Sundering. No one woke from that catastrophe still speaking it, and no
living race speaks it today. It survives only in writing, on the walls and
tablets of Atral.
It did not vanish entirely, however. Just as the descendant races inherited
their progenitors' attributes in muted and partial form, the languages that
emerged after the Sundering carry the bones of the Drasian tongue beneath very
different skin. Nheqethi, Aiwiya, Antathi, Stone Speak, Andari and the rest
share structures, root forms, and habits of construction that all trace back to
the same silenced source. No speaker of any one of them would recognize the
kinship — the daughter tongues are far too changed, and too changed from one
another. The relationship is visible only to someone holding many of them side
by side against the written original, which is the work the Var Harma have been
doing at Atral for centuries. See Kingdoms_History.
The languages of the Draak-descended races stand outside this family entirely,
having fractured from a different progenitor.

DRAAK
Most closely resembled present-day dragons but larger and more powerful.
Capable of flight. Intelligent and capable of magic, but not to the extent of
the Drasu. Central heartland in the Black Mountains of Fardar (Draak Territory).
The potential for both good and evil existed within the Draak — same as Drasu.
Split into dragons, dragonborne, wyrms, kobolds, and other types.
The Draak were active, willing participants in monitoring and protecting the
Abaculus during its first hiding place in the Black Mountains — lending their
physical strength, flight, and magical abilities to that purpose. Their
custodial role ended when Ah'Tabai retrieved the Abaculus prior to the Battle
of Atral. All Draak were shattered in the Sundering that followed.
ANCIENT DRAAK SETTLEMENT: The Draak maintained a settlement in the river valley
dividing the eastern and western halves of the Black Mountains — the heart of
their territory. Not a city in the Drasian sense — the Draak were not builders
of that sophistication. More likely a gathering place of natural caves and peaks
modified and expanded to suit their size, with wide passages, open roosting
ledges, and fire-scorched communal spaces. The scale would be enormous by modern
standards given the Draak's size, but crude compared to Atral. The name of this
settlement is undeveloped. What remains of it after thousands of years — and
whether the Iron Dwarves of Fardar have encountered any of it during their
mining operations — is undeveloped.

1ST EPOCH TERRITORIES (confirmed on maps):
- Drasian Empire: Northern Edura, centered on Atral along the Waywin River.
- Draak Territory: Black Mountains of Fardar and surrounding region, central
  Alios. Not a conventional kingdom — a roosting ground and heartland.
Outside these two, the rest of Lanamyr was mostly wild and untamed.

MOUNTAIN TROLL ORIGIN (confirmed):
Troll's Breath Mountains, just south of the ruins of Atral and the Waywin
River on Edura. Emerged there at the Sundering and have never truly left.
The mountain range is named after them. They contested the Atral ruins with
the High Elves during early excavations and were pushed back by the combined
High Elf and Sand Dwarf alliance. They remain a periodic threat to excavation
operations — they know the ruins contain things of value. Extensive underground
caverns originate in the Troll's Breath Mountains; some connect into the outer
edges of the underground ruins of Atral. Hostile contact with Var Harma
excavations occurs periodically when their paths intersect underground. The
Trolls have at most found remnants of buried structures on the outer edges of
Atral — nowhere near the Drasian Vault at the city's center.

TIERS (where a tier is used): Minimal, Low, Average, High, Maximum.

================================================================================
TWO CLASSES OF INHERITANCE — READ FIRST
================================================================================

Everything a race woke up with came through the Sundering from its progenitor.
But inherited qualities fall into two distinct classes, and conflating them
will produce errors.

CLASS ONE — MANDATE FRAGMENTS. Exclusive and binary. A race either holds the
fragment or holds none of it; there is no lesser version in anyone else. These
are the shattered pieces of the Drasu caretaker mandate, and they are
relationships with the living world rather than capabilities. Not tiered,
because there is nothing to tier — the races that do not hold a given fragment
hold zero of it.
  Confirmed: Wood Elves (forest and growing things), Shepherds (animals, at the
  scale of the individual creature), Water Whisperers (sea life, at the scale
  of the whole ocean). See the DRASU entry.

CLASS TWO — UNIVERSAL INHERITANCES. Present in EVERY race to some degree and
unevenly distributed. The named race is not the only one that has it; it is the
one with the strongest expression of it. Everyone else has some. These are
tiered, and the existing framework traits of MAGIC and INGENUITY are already
members of this class — every race has magical capacity in diminished form and
every race has some rate of invention, they simply differ in degree.
  Further members confirmed, to be developed with their races:
  - INVENTIVENESS / INGENUITY — strongest expression: SKY GNOMES.
  - AGRICULTURE AND CULTIVATION — strongest expression: PLAINS ELVES. Growing
    and tending, intuitive diagnosis of what ails a crop, and deliberate
    selective breeding. See their extended entry.
  - FORGING AND METALWORK — a universal inheritance. Strongest holder not yet
    determined.
  Others will be added as races are developed.

THE DISTINCTION MATTERS MOST WHERE THE TWO LOOK ALIKE. Wood Elf forest
stewardship and Plains Elf agriculture both concern growing things and are
NOT the same kind of thing. The Wood Elf trait is a mandate fragment: exclusive,
a stewardship relationship with wild forest, held by no other race in any
degree. Plains Elf agriculture is a universal inheritance: cultivation for use,
present in every race that farms, expressed most strongly here. A race can be a
superb farmer without holding any fragment at all, and most are.

A race's TECHNOLOGY BASELINE is in large part the visible output of its Class
Two inheritances at the moment of waking — a race with strong inherited forging
woke with skilled forging in its hands.

MAGIC — fixed and absolute.
A race's magical capacity derives from its progenitor inheritance, carried in
diminished form through the Sundering. It is innate, it does not drift over
time, and it cannot be taught to a race, traded for, or lost in a war. Because
the baseline never moves, no era needs to be specified. See Overview_Cosmology,
Magic System, for the Magni exception.

TECHNOLOGY BASELINE — fixed, set at the opening of the 2nd Epoch.
What a race woke up already knowing how to do. This is INHERITED KNOWLEDGE AND
MUSCLE MEMORY, not learning — craft carried in the hands as much as the mind,
present from the first day without anyone having been taught. The Sundering
preserved residual intelligence — language, reasoning, and the capacity to
build and organize — so the baseline is simply how much practical craft
survived in that race's fragment. Nobody taught anyone anything. Recorded as a
description rather than a tier.
RELATION TO OTHER INHERITANCES: magic capacity, the caretaker fragments, and
the technology baseline are all inheritances carried through the Sundering, and
in practice they are intertwined. What separates them is that craft knowledge
can afterward be taught, traded, advanced, and lost, while magic capacity and a
caretaker fragment cannot move between races at all. See the Drasu entry for
the caretaker mandate.

INGENUITY — fixed tier. The rate of technological progress, measured against
all of Lanamyr rather than regionally.
Ingenuity sets the CEILING on how fast a race can advance. Actual progress is
gated by circumstance, and four gates apply:
  - RESOURCES. A craft cannot develop without the materials it requires.
  - SURPLUS. Specialists require others to feed them. A small population has no
    spare labor for invention.
  - CONTACT. Isolated peoples stagnate; connected ones compound. Contact between
    races is a technological event as well as a political one.
  - CATASTROPHE. War, plague, and collapse push a people backward regardless of
    ingenuity. Knowledge can be lost — see Nusul in Kingdoms_History.
Because progress is gated, technological history is uneven and not always
forward-moving. A race may forget nothing and still fall behind as others
advance. Technology transfers between races through contact and conquest;
magic never does.
No specific dates are assigned to a race crossing from one level of capability
to another. Technological eras are described organically within each
civilization's history rather than pinned to a timeline.

COHESION — an outcome, not an inherent trait.
Whether a race acts as a single polity or as many. Determined during the
scatter-and-consolidation phase at the opening of the 2nd Epoch (see
Overview_Cosmology, The Scatter Pattern) and rarely changed afterward. Cohesion
determines whether diplomacy with a race is even possible and whether an
agreement can bind anyone beyond the individual who made it.

OUTSIDER DISPOSITION — the cultural default on meeting a stranger. Described
rather than tiered. Drifts slowly.

WESTERN ALIOS — RECORDED VALUES:

WOOD ELVES
  Magic: High
  Technology baseline: Accomplished woodworking — felling, shaping, joinery,
    and construction in timber; bows, hafts, carts, boats, framed buildings;
    cordage and woven fiber. Simple forging: able to heat, hammer, and shape
    metal into serviceable tools and blades, but plain work with no refinement,
    alloying, or mastery of temper. Stone and bone tools in common early use.
  Ingenuity: Low
  Cohesion: High
  Outsider disposition: Guarded at first contact, integrative over time. By the
    3rd Epoch Faelyn houses humans, gnomes, and other races.

DARK DWARVES
  Magic: Average
  Technology baseline: Skilled forging well beyond simple shaping — control of
    heat, quality, and finish, producing tools and weapons of real quality.
    Expert mining: shaft-sinking, following a seam, moving stone safely
    underground. Fine stonework, structural and worked. Weak at woodworking,
    which they treat as a material to acquire rather than a craft they practice.
  Ingenuity: Average — perfecters rather than inventors, refining a known craft
    across centuries.
  Cohesion: High
  Outsider disposition: Transactional. The only one of the three western Alios
    races that travels TO others rather than waiting to be found.

STONE GIANTS
  Magic: Minimal
  Technology baseline: Clubs and thrown stone. Hide and bone worked by hand.
    Shelter is caves where the mountains provide them and crude huts where they
    do not — logs stacked and leaned rather than joined, roofed with hide or
    boughs. No metalworking at all.
  Ingenuity: Minimal
  Cohesion: Low — clans and holdings, never a unified people. No Stone Giant
    kingdom exists in any epoch.
  Outsider disposition: Aloof and dismissive when fed; dangerous when crowded.
    The boreal rain forest supplies nearly everything they need, so they have
    little reason to deal with anyone.

NOTE ON MINIMAL TECHNOLOGY AND MILITARY THREAT: a Minimal technology tier does
not mean a race is militarily weak. Stone Giant size makes a thrown stone a
siege weapon and a club a decisive one. Their bodies are their technology.

NOTE ON HEIGHT CONVENTIONS:
- Hillfolk humans are the baseline standard — equivalent to real-world humans.
- All human subtypes share variety in hair, eye color, and skin tone. They are
  distinguished primarily by physical size and geographic origin, not appearance.
- Dwarf gender height separation is 1 inch (male taller) unless otherwise noted.
- Gnome male and female heights are equal unless otherwise noted.
- Magni follow the physical appearance of their base race — see footnote.

NOTE ON LANGUAGE NAMING:
Present-day language names reflect the world as it exists in 432 AW and may not
reflect what those languages were called in earlier epochs. Andari, for example,
is named for the Andar region — a regional identity the early 2nd Epoch Northmen
would not yet have had. This convention likely applies to other race languages
as well. Native language acquisition is universal to the race. Additional language
knowledge is individual — determined by travels, education, and experience, not
race. Common is widely spoken as a second language across most of Lanamyr.

MAGNI FOOTNOTE: Magni are not a separate race. They are born into any race as
an exceedingly rare fluke. Their physical appearance follows their base race
entirely. The table below does not include a Magni entry for this reason.
The nature of the fluke: descendant races inherited their progenitors' magical
capacity in muted form at the Sundering. A Magni receives it undiminished —
full Drasu capacity for the Drasu-descended races, full Draak capacity for the
Draak-descended races. The Draak held a lesser mastery of magic than the Drasu,
so a Draak-descended Magni has a lower ceiling than a Drasu-descended one —
still powerful, but with less potential. A Magni inherits magical capacity only,
not the mind or mental resilience of their progenitors.
See Overview & Cosmology, Magic System.

KOBOLD SUBTYPES
================================================================================

Four confirmed Kobold subtypes, all descended from the Draak. As with all
races, Kobolds have spread throughout Lanamyr over the 2nd and 3rd Epochs —
any subtype may be encountered in travels far from their origin territory.

MYSTIC KOBOLDS
Origin: Southern Alios, northwest of the Obsidian Dragonborne swamp region.
Lifespan: 50 years — longest of all Kobold subtypes.
Appearance: Taller and leaner than most Kobolds. Shorter tails.
Traits: The most intelligent Kobold subtype. One of the rare subtypes with
natural magical capability — magic is possible but still uncommon even among
Mystics. Skitterskink (Skitter) is a Mystic Kobold. Their greater intelligence
and capacity for magic has allowed many Mystics to move beyond tribal life and
integrate into the wider world more readily than other subtypes.

TUNNELER KOBOLDS
Origin: Far eastern end of the Black Mountains of Fardar, within Fardar's
borders but distant enough from the kingdom's center to avoid significant
conflict with the Iron Dwarves.
Lifespan: 40 years.
Appearance: Shorter and more powerfully built than other Kobolds. Large hands
with sharp claws adapted for digging.
Traits: Average intelligence by Kobold standards. Cave and burrow dwellers —
their origin in the Black Mountains suited their subterranean lifestyle. Their
proximity to the ancient Draak settlement in the river valley means they may
have encountered Draak remnants that the Iron Dwarves have not yet found.

SCAVENGER KOBOLDS
Origin: Southern tip of Edura. Cold, harsh tundra environment.
Lifespan: 35 years — shortest of all Kobold subtypes.
Appearance: Smaller than other Kobolds. Fast and agile.
Traits: Hunt and operate in packs. Very ferocious — seldom use weapons,
preferring claws and teeth. Opportunistic and animalistic in behavior, targeting
farms and livestock primarily. Generally cowardly unless numbers strongly favor
them. The harsh cold environment of their origin shaped their pack survival
instincts. Short lifespan reflects the attrition of their feral lifestyle.

HARDBACK KOBOLDS
Origin: Evergreen Strand, far eastern tip of Utoa.
Lifespan: 40 years.
Appearance: The most physically imposing Kobold subtype. Thicker hide
throughout, particularly on the back where they have larger and thicker
dragon-like scales — the feature that gives them their name and their notable
durability.
Traits: The least intelligent Kobold subtype. Brutish and reckless. Their
tough hide makes them considerably harder to harm than other Kobolds.

================================================================================
GOBLIN SUBTYPES
================================================================================

Three confirmed Goblin subtypes, all descended from the Drasu. As with all
races, Goblins have spread throughout Lanamyr over the 2nd and 3rd Epochs —
any subtype may be encountered in travels far from their origin territory.

SCREECHER GOBLINS
Origin: Wandering Hills, northeastern Kimora, northern Alios.
Lifespan: 15 years — the shortest lifespan of any race in all of Lanamyr.
Traits: Rudimentary language and speech capability. When excited or frightened,
emit very high-pitched shrieks — the feature that defines and names them. The
brevity of their lifespan shapes a fast and chaotic existence.

MARSH GOBLINS
Origin: Small island off the eastern coast of Alios. Island name undeveloped.
Lifespan: 30 years — longest of all Goblin subtypes.
Traits: Build small earthen huts. The most socially developed Goblin subtype —
a more noticeable social structure than the other subtypes. Reasonably
intelligent. Their island origin may have contributed to their greater social
cohesion and development in relative isolation.

FERAL GOBLINS
Origin: The region between the Ash Mountains to the north and the Blackwing
Hills to the south, south of Kimora.
Lifespan: 20 years.
Traits: The most common Goblin subtype. Very savage and territorial. Capable
of forging and creating weapons and armor, though the quality is very poor.
The name refers to their demeanor rather than their intelligence or
craftsmanship — they are not incapable, simply brutal and aggressive by nature.

================================================================================
ICE DWARVES — EXPANSION AND ICE FORGING
================================================================================

EXPANSION BEYOND ICEFALL:
After several centuries of settling Icefall, the Ice Dwarves felt the need to
expand beyond their island kingdom for additional resources. In year 221 of the
2nd Epoch, the first major expeditions departed Icefall in multiple directions.

Sailing south, they found the Tempest Peaks and the region of the Darkened
Slopes. They found much of that region already occupied but established trade
routes there. These routes helped build friendly relations with the neighboring
peoples over time.

THE SEQUENCE (confirmed, v006): the southern fleet made landfall first at the
northern end of the western arc, on the edges of Stone Giant territory. Those
encounters were tense and any peace was short-lived — Stone Giant cohesion is
Low and no institution exists there capable of holding an agreement past the
life of a chief. The Ice Dwarves declined to settle and continued south and west
along the outer coast until they found unoccupied ground. They settled, and
shortly afterward met the Dark Dwarves. That settlement became KULDOR, now one
of the largest port cities in Alios — see Geography. The friendly relations the
passage above describes are with the Dark Dwarves and in time the Wood Elves,
NOT with the Giants.

Another expedition sailing in a different direction discovered the Tarantar
Mountains on northern Alios and recognized the abundance of mineral wealth
there. It sent word back to Icefall, and after eighteen years of deliberation
and preparation a contingent sailed to found Nusul in 239. The two expeditions
were INDEPENDENT and remained so — the southern settlement played no part in the
discovery of the Tarantars. See Kingdoms_History file.
THE ASYMMETRY: Icefall lost the northern venture entirely in 268 and kept the
southern one for the next twenty-four centuries. From 268 onward Kuldor is
Icefall's only foreign holding.

ICE DWARF RELATIONS WITH DARK DWARVES:
Close, and far closer than with the Iron Dwarves — but the closeness is
PRACTICAL rather than sentimental. Shared Drasu heritage produces nothing by
itself. What produced this relationship was contact history and complementary
craft, beginning in 221 and compounding for millennia in a shared city.
A TENSION WORTH HOLDING: Icefall is Polar Ice and has no timber of its own
either, so two mining peoples arrived at the same forest. Whether Ice Dwarf sea
haul eventually relieved the Dark Dwarves' timber problem or priced them out of
their own trade is UNDEVELOPED. What it cannot do is break the fuel deadlock —
the Wood Elves' refusal to sell charcoal is not a transport problem.
What Icefall itself burns, having no timber, is UNDEVELOPED.

ICE DWARF RELATIONS WITH IRON DWARVES:
Ice Dwarves and Iron Dwarves occasionally cross paths in the lands between their
kingdoms, but the two kingdoms have no strong direct ties. Trade between them is
indirect — goods pass through intermediary kingdoms rather than through direct
exchange. The two cultures share ancient Drasu heritage but have developed very
differently over the centuries since the Sundering.

ICE FORGING:
A unique weapon-crafting tradition specific to Icefall. Ice Forged weapons are
highly sought after across Lanamyr and carry significant trade value. The
technique cannot be replicated elsewhere. Three factors combine to make it
exclusive to Icefall:

THE ORE: Iron mined from the mountains of Icefall has properties not found in
iron ore from other regions. Superior hardness and durability are inherent to
the raw material. Taking Ice Forging technique to another location and using
foreign ore will not produce the same result.

THE ENVIRONMENT: The quenching process requires extreme cold temperatures that
exist naturally on Icefall but are not found in almost any other part of
Lanamyr. The cold itself is the quenching medium — not water or oil.

THE TECHNIQUE: After the metal is heated and shaped, it is quenched in the
extreme cold. The protected secret lies entirely in the quenching process —
the precise timing, the duration of cold exposure, and the controlled rate at
which the blade's temperature is dropped. These variables, applied correctly
to Icefall ore in Icefall conditions, produce a blade that requires no
tempering. In conventional forging, a blade must be reheated slightly after
quenching — a process called tempering — to prevent brittleness. Ice Forged
blades skip this step entirely and are not brittle after quenching. They hold
their edge remarkably well.

The knowledge is embodied — carried in the hands and instincts of living smiths,
passed down through generations of Ice Dwarf craftspeople. It is not recorded
in any book or vault. This makes the tradition both protected and vulnerable:
it cannot be stolen from a document, but it could be lost if the smiths who
carry it were ever decimated or isolated.

Any smith attempting to replicate Ice Forging without all three elements —
the ore, the environment, and the precise technique — would produce blades
that shatter or perform no better than standard work.

SHEPHERDS
Origin: Distant island on the far eastern edge of the world map.
The Shepherds partly get their name for their deep, symbiotic relationship with
animals. Their existence on their island was and remains one of balance with the
natural world around them. By the 3rd Epoch they have spread throughout most of
Lanamyr, as have most races.
ANIMAL AFFINITY: Shepherds possess a deep affinity with all animals that goes
beyond learned behavior. They have a profound intuitive understanding of animal
behavior, body language, threat signals, and how animals perceive friends and
danger. This alone would make them exceptional — but there is something deeper
at play, something fundamental to the Shepherds that other humans simply do not
possess. If a wild wolf encounters an ordinary Hillfolk human it is likely to
attack. A Shepherd might calm it entirely. The Shepherds themselves do not have
a complete explanation for this quality.
THE ACTUAL ORIGIN (confirmed; unknown in-world): the affinity is a fragment of
the Drasu caretaker mandate. The Drasu were meant to tend Lanamyr in the
absence of the Irridae, and at the Sundering that role fractured unevenly
across the descendant races. The Shepherds received the animal portion of it,
as the Wood Elves received the forest portion. Neither race knows this, and
neither can recognize the other as holding a matching piece. See the Drasu
entry above and Overview_Cosmology.
Note that no other human subtype carries it — Hillfolk, Northmen and Water
Whisperers did not receive this fragment. It descended to the Shepherds as a
race, not to humans as a family.
CONSEQUENCE FOR THE DIET: the vegetarianism described below is not merely a
cultural norm layered on top of the affinity. It follows from the inheritance
itself. A people carrying a fragment of a mandate to tend living things do not
eat what they were made to keep, and to most Shepherds it does not feel like a
choice because in a sense it never was one.
COMPANION ANIMALS: Shepherds are almost without exception accompanied by an
animal companion of some kind. The word "pet" is misleading — the relationship
is more accurately described as a partnership. The animal chose to be there, and
the Shepherd chose it back. This mutual selection reflects the deeper nature of
how Shepherds relate to the animal world.
GUIDES: Shepherds are frequently sought out as wilderness guides. Moving through
terrain with a Shepherd is a fundamentally different experience — animals that
would be threats to others are non-threats or even assets. A Shepherd reads
terrain through animal behavior in ways no other guide can replicate.
DIET: Shepherds are vegetarian as a cultural norm. For a people with genuine
partnerships with animals and that deeper affinity, eating them is a fundamental
contradiction of everything they are. It does not feel like a choice to most
Shepherds — it simply is not something they do. A Shepherd traveling far from
home in a region where plant-based options are scarce may adapt out of necessity
rather than choice.
Native language: Tapyr.

WATER WHISPERERS
Origin: Small island cluster to the southwest of the main Alios/Edura landmass,
south of the Malulo Archipelago region.

SEA AFFINITY (confirmed): the Water Whisperers carry a fragment of the Drasu
caretaker mandate, as the Wood Elves and Shepherds do — theirs is an affinity
with sea life. It is innate rather than learned, it cannot be taught to anyone
else, and like the other fragments it is not understood in-world. The name the
rest of Lanamyr gave them is closer to the truth than anyone realizes.
HOW IT DIFFERS FROM THE SHEPHERDS: the Shepherd fragment works at the scale of
the INDIVIDUAL — a bond with a particular animal, mutually chosen, carried as a
partnership. The Water Whisperer fragment works at the scale of the WHOLE. They
read the sea as a living system: where life is and why, how a shoal moves, what
a change in the water means, which grounds are thriving and which are spent.
They do not befriend a fish. They understand an ocean.
CONSEQUENCE — WHY THE FISHING IS NOT A CONTRADICTION: Water Whisperers are
confirmed skilled fishermen, which would sit badly with a caretaker fragment if
theirs worked the way the Shepherds' does. It does not. One can tend a whole
and still take from it; a fishery is kept by being managed rather than by being
left alone. Their take is restrained, informed, and directed away from grounds
that cannot bear it — not out of regulation but because they can feel the
difference. This is the same shape as Wood Elf practice, which fells timber and
replants at the margins, and the opposite of the Shepherd resolution, which was
to stop eating animals entirely. Three fragments, three different relationships
with the living thing in question.
CONSEQUENCE — THE FISHING SKILL: their reputation as skilled fishermen is not
purely craft. Knowing where the fish are is the affinity, not the technique.

The most accomplished seafaring civilization in all of Lanamyr. Their shipbuilding
is unmatched anywhere in the world. Unlike Ice Forging — which is locked to
Icefall by its ore, environment, and technique — Water Whisperer shipbuilding
travels with the people. A Water Whisperer shipwright can build an exceptional
vessel anywhere in Lanamyr. Their skill lives entirely in the craftspeople
themselves, making them highly sought after in any port city. They are also
skilled fishermen. By the 3rd Epoch they have spread throughout most of Lanamyr.
Native language: Hikri.

ELF SUBTYPES — EXTENDED ENTRIES

ELVES — GENERAL
All elf subtypes share certain physical characteristics: pointed ears and more
delicate features than most other races. They tend toward leaner and more slender
builds than Hillfolk humans, though this varies by subtype. Elves have finite
lifespans — they are not immortal. Elderly elves exist in the world.

WOOD ELVES
Origin: Forested lowlands south of the Tempest Peaks, western arm of Alios.
Kingdom: Faelyn. Capital: Caldalus.
The biome map records their origin region as Cool Temperate Moist Forest, with
Cool Temperate Wet Forest to either side, grading to Boreal Wet Forest at the
northern foothills and to Subtropical Dry Forest at the southern margin. They
woke in forest and have never lived anywhere else.
Native language: Aiwiya.

MINIMAL-IMPACT PHILOSOPHY: Wood Elves do not clear large swaths of forest.
They fell only what a structure requires and build among the larger trees left
standing. This is a deliberate ethic, not an inability.
ORIGIN OF THE ETHIC — INHERITED, NOT LEARNED (confirmed): the affinity for the
natural world and the impulse to protect the trees came down from the Drasu,
who were meant to be the caretakers of Lanamyr in the absence of the Irridae.
The Wood Elves did not arrive at it by reasoning or by remorse. They woke with
it. See the Drasu entry above.
The practical shape of their building has a separate and more mundane driver:
in dense forest a threat cannot be seen coming at ground level, so the earliest
bands built upward for safety, and for protection from flooding and predators.
Inherited disposition and practical necessity happened to point the same way.
NO GUILT, NO SHAME — this is important to the texture of the race. Wood Elves
understood from the beginning that their need for raw material would have some
impact on the forest, and they accepted it as the cost of living there. What
follows from the inherited affinity is not remorse but effort: they work to
minimize the impact, and where they can, to offset it. They do not agonize over
the clearings. They account for them.
WHAT YEARS 30 TO 60 ACTUALLY MARK: not the birth of the ethic, which was never
absent, but its formalization. As expansion grew and the first substantial
clearings became permanent features, an innate disposition had to become
articulated practice — what may be taken, in what quantity, and what is owed
back. TENTATIVE framing; the dating is carried over from v004.

REPLANTING (confirmed): smaller Wood Elf settlements near the edges of the
forest plant trees in open spaces. This is active restoration rather than mere
restraint, and it is the clearest expression of the inherited mandate in
everyday practice.
GEOGRAPHIC CONSEQUENCE: carried out at the margins across centuries, this
holds and in places extends the forest boundary — against the Subtropical Dry
Forest at Faelyn's southern margin and the Boreal Wet Forest at the northern
foothills. The Wood Elf forest is not merely preserved. It is tended.
CONTRAST WITH THE DARK DWARVES: the Dark Dwarves stripped the slow-growing
boreal woodland on their own margins and did not replant, and boreal forest
recovers poorly. Over the same centuries one people's forest held or grew while
the other's did not come back. This is a visible difference on the land at the
boundary between them, and it is a substantial part of why the Dark Dwarves
eventually came north looking for timber at all. See Kingdoms_History, THE
BINDING TRADE.

HEAVEN'S OAK IN WOOD ELF BELIEF: the Oak stands at the center of Caldalus and
its true purpose is unknown to all but the Guardians of the Tree. Many Wood
Elves hold some variation of the belief that it is the seed of their creation,
or that it may unlock the memories lost from before. None of it is true, and
attempts on those memories at the Oak have never reached beyond the Sundering
boundary. See Geography, HEAVEN'S OAK, and Overview_Cosmology, MEMORIES.

CANOPY ARCHITECTURE: Wood Elf settlements are built high among the trees —
networks of intricate, very well-built structures linked by walkways and
bridges. The wet climate of the western arm is what makes a timber canopy city
survivable; in the Subtropical Dry Forest at Faelyn's southern margin the style
thins out considerably, and ground construction is proportionally more common
there.
THE CLEARINGS — ORDER OF CONSTRUCTION (confirmed): the canopy came FIRST. The
earliest Wood Elf structures were built in the treetops, and ground
construction followed afterward rather than preceding it.
As canopy settlements expanded they demanded ever greater quantities of raw
material, and the gathering and hauling of it produced the first substantial
clearings in the forest. Those clearings were then built into as well. Wood
Elves did not clear land in order to build; they cleared it incidentally, in
the course of building upward, and then used what they had made. Over centuries
this produced settlements that are both canopy and ground, including Caldalus —
but the canopy is always the older layer. See Geography.

METAL — THREE CEILINGS: Wood Elf metalworking is permanently small in scale,
for three independent reasons that all land in the same place.
  - POOR ORE. Forested lowland yields no minable ore. What they have is bog
    iron, gathered from forest seeps and marsh bottoms rather than mined —
    scarce and of poor quality. Stone and bone tools filled the gap in the
    early centuries and remain in use for many purposes.
  - NO FUEL THEY WILL BURN. Smelting is the single largest consumer of wood of
    any craft. Producing charcoal at scale is incompatible with the
    minimal-impact ethic, and they decline to do it.
  - MAGIC SUBSTITUTES ONLY AT TINY SCALE. High magic provides an alternate heat
    source for smelting, but it is extremely inefficient and wholly impractical
    as a permanent method. A mage must continuously produce the heat for the
    duration rather than lighting a fire that burns on its own. It is sufficient
    for a small crucible and a few pounds of bog iron and useless for sustained
    or industrial work.
CONSEQUENCE: Wood Elf metalworking is a magical profession rather than a
mechanical one — a smith in Faelyn is a spellcaster who works metal, not a
craftsman who owns a forge. Metal goods are individually costly and therefore
rare and significant rather than everyday. This is why Wood Elf forging remains
"simple" into the 3rd Epoch despite millennia of trade with master smiths: they
did not fail to develop metallurgy, they declined to. See Characters & Items
regarding Crimson and Sear — whether they are of Wood Elf make is UNDECIDED.

PLAINS ELVES
Origin: Central steppes of Alios. Kingdom: Ellendale.
Native language: Antathi. Lifespan 200.
TERRAIN OF ORIGIN (verified against the biome map, v005): Cool Temperate
Steppes, read unambiguously at the origin point with no competing biome
nearby. Rainfall is moderate — wetter than arid country, drier than the
western arm. Temperature is cool temperate, close to Faelyn's. This is deep,
fertile, treeless grassland with a single annual growing season, and it is the
best grain country in Lanamyr. It provides no timber whatsoever.

THE AGRICULTURAL INHERITANCE (confirmed): Plains Elves hold the strongest
expression of the agriculture and cultivation inheritance. This is a CLASS TWO
universal inheritance, not a mandate fragment — every race farms to some
degree, the Plains Elves simply do it better than anyone. They are not
stewards of wild growth the way the Wood Elves are, and the two should not be
confused. See TWO CLASSES OF INHERITANCE.
  - INTUITIVE DIAGNOSIS. They identify what is wrong with a crop far faster
    than others can — blight, deficiency, pest, water, soil. It presents as
    intuition rather than analysis. They frequently cannot say how they know.
  - SELECTIVE BREEDING. They deliberately practice selective breeding of crops,
    carried out as sustained programs rather than casual seed-saving. The
    surrounding nations know that the largest and best-tasting produce in the
    region comes from Ellendale.

WHY NO ONE CATCHES UP — TWO FACTORS, NOT ONE: the inheritance alone would not
produce this, and neither would the lifespan. It takes both.
  - THE INHERITANCE supplies the judgment: which plant to carry forward, and
    the fast reading of what is wrong.
  - THE LIFESPAN supplies the time horizon, and it is decisive. The steppe
    yields one crop generation per year. A Plains Elf adult has roughly 150
    productive years and can therefore run 150 consecutive generations of
    selection on a single crop PERSONALLY — one continuous program, one set of
    judgments, no handoff. A Hillfolk human farmer gets perhaps fifty seasons
    and must hand the work to an heir who did not see the earlier ones.
    Every handoff loses intent. The Plains Elves have none.
This is the same structural logic as Wood Elf cohesion: a long lifespan buys
something no amount of effort buys in a short one.

TRADE POSITION: Ellendale sits midway along The Road, the heaviest trade route
in central Alios, with Kimora and Fardar to the east and Faelyn to the west.
Their produce moves in both directions. A kingdom that feeds its neighbors
holds a lever over them — worth bearing in mind alongside Lord Yllyndel the
Ancient's political protection of the Herringer resistance under Bourbac.
See Kingdoms_History.

WHY THE SEED IS NOT THE SECRET (confirmed): a superior crop variety reproduces
itself, and anyone sold produce from Ellendale has effectively been sold its
seeds. The seeds do travel, and no attempt is made to stop them. They are the
least important of the factors, and the advantage does not live in them.
Three things make Ellendale produce what it is, and only one of them can be
carried away in a pocket. The structure parallels Ice Forging — see the Ice
Dwarf entry — where the technique is useless without the ore and the cold.

  - THE SOIL. The central plains are richer in nutrients than the land
    surrounding them. This is a property of the place and cannot be exported
    with a seed any more than Icefall's ore can be carried to a warm forge.
  - THE GROWER. The innate skill and intuition of the Plains Elf — the tending,
    the care, the fast reading of what a plant needs before it visibly
    declines. This is the inheritance, and it is the one factor that is
    strictly impossible to transfer. It cannot be taught, bought, or copied,
    and no amount of practice will give it to someone who did not inherit it.
  - THE PROTECTION. Plains Elves take a very active role in pest and small
    animal control to defend their yield. This is sustained, organized,
    deliberate work rather than an occasional response to infestation, and a
    great deal of a harvest's quality is simply the portion that was never lost.

CONSEQUENCE: a farmer elsewhere planting Ellendale seed gets a decent crop and
not an exceptional one, and concludes either that the elves held something back
or that the seed degenerated away from home. Neither is true. He has the seed
and none of the three things that mattered. This mirrors the fate of any smith
attempting Ice Forging without the ore and the cold.

CONTRAST WORTH NOTING — PLAINS ELVES AND SHEPHERDS: the Plains Elves kill small
animals in sustained, organized numbers to protect a harvest. The Shepherds
hold a mandate fragment for animals and will not eat one. Both are inheritances
concerning living things, and they point in opposite directions. A Shepherd
travelling in Ellendale would find the pest control genuinely difficult to
witness. This is the clearest available illustration that tending does not mean
refraining, and that these inheritances are not variations on one temperament.

SNOW ELVES
Origin: Large island just east of Utoa, close enough to be considered part of
the continent. Island name and kingdom name undeveloped.
The Snow Elves diverge most dramatically from the typical elf silhouette. Their
extreme environment has shaped them into something that looks almost alien among
their own kind. Both male and female average 5'1" — the shortest elf subtype,
with no meaningful height difference between genders. Their build is thick and
powerful, with strong legs built for trudging through deep snow — very unlike the
lean slender frame of most elves. Their skin is pallid, sometimes with a faint
hint of blue. Their hair is dark — blacks and dark tones — contrasting sharply
with their eyes, which are strikingly bright and light in color. The combination
of dark hair and vivid eyes against pale skin makes them immediately distinctive.
Native language: Tuavasy.

DARK ELVES
Origin: Beneath the Tarantar Mountains, northern Alios. Kingdom: The Deep
(underground). Their skin is darker gray tones, sometimes bordering on hints of
deep purple — similar at a glance to Dark Dwarves but meaningfully different on
closer inspection. Their hair is generally very light silver, gray, or white.
Their eyes appear cloudy, with irises typically gray or pale blue — giving the
impression of compromised vision to those unfamiliar with them. In reality their
eyes are adapted to the low light of The Deep. Dark Elves spend the majority of
their lives underground and are more reclusive than most surface races. They
are more surface-averse than Dark Dwarves.
Native language: Ukuri.

SEA ELVES
Origin: Malulo Archipelago, western ocean. Kingdom: Malulo.
Immediately distinctive among all races. Their skin is aqua in tone — light
blue-green, the color of shallow tropical water. Their hair matches: blues and
greens, the colors of the sea. Their build is lean and slender, suited for
swimming. The majority of Sea Elf civilization exists underwater — the islands
of the Malulo Archipelago are only the surface-visible portion of their society.
TENTATIVE: approximately 90% of Sea Elf presence is underwater, 10% on land —
not yet confirmed. Nature of underwater settlements undeveloped.
Native language: Vhitoshi.

DWARF SUBTYPES — EXTENDED ENTRIES
================================================================================

SAND DWARVES
Origin: Void Sands Desert, central Edura.
A lifetime under the brutal desert sun has left its mark on the Sand Dwarves.
Their skin is brown to dark bronze in tone and carries a weathered, rough,
leathery texture — not just a color but a quality earned by generations of
exposure to the desert heat. Their hair runs in browns and blond tones, matching
their arid environment. Their eyes are generally lighter browns and hazel with
some variety. They have allied historically with the High Elves in the
excavation and defense of the ruins of Atral.
Native language: Obathi.

IRON DWARVES
Origin: Black Mountains of Fardar, central Alios. Kingdom: Fardar.
The tallest of all dwarf subtypes, averaging 4'11" for males and 4'10" for
females. Their build is compact and powerful. Unlike most dwarf subtypes, Iron
Dwarves show significant variety in skin tone, hair color, and eye color —
individual Iron Dwarves can look quite different from one another. Bor
Glitterbeard has reddish-orange hair and hazel eyes; Grimmon Herringer had dark
brown hair and blue eyes. Their native language is called Stone Speak — a
colloquial name coined by the Iron Dwarves themselves rather than a formal
linguistic title. Two major periods of enslavement — under Bourbac and under
Tarik — have left a lingering cultural distrust of humans.
Native language: Stone Speak.

DARK DWARVES
Origin: Forbidden Peaks and Darkened Slopes, western arm of Alios.
Kingdom: Hellar (underground city in the Forbidden Peaks).
Dark Dwarves are similar to Dark Elves in coloring at first glance — darker
skin complexions and lighter colored hair. However their eyes are not clouded
and are generally darker in tone. Unlike Dark Elves, Dark Dwarves are not
deeply reclusive and spend more time above ground, though they are still
effectively blinded by daylight without protection. Their greater surface
presence makes them more commonly encountered than Dark Elves.
Native language: Tecali.

TERRAIN OF ORIGIN: The biome map records their origin as Cool Temperate
Steppes — open, dry, treeless high country, and the driest corner of the
western arm. Warm Temperate Thorn Scrub lies to the south in the Forbidden
Peaks; Boreal Moist Forest lies along the northern Darkened Slopes and inland
to the east. They are the only one of the three western Alios races to have
woken in open country.

DAYLIGHT SENSITIVITY IS ACQUIRED, NOT INNATE: Dark Dwarves woke at the
Sundering with normal eyesight. On open steppe offering no shade and no
shelter, the only refuge available was caves, and their natural craftsmanship
and skill drew them steadily deeper underground. The sensitivity to daylight
developed afterward, over generations of subterranean life, as a latent
predisposition expressing itself rather than as ordinary selection.
This distinguishes them from the races that woke underground. Dark Elves and
Cave Gnomes both emerged beneath the Tarantar Mountains and never had sight to
lose; Dark Dwarves gave theirs up. It is why they remain the least sensitive of
the underground races and the only one that still travels and trades in person.
CONSEQUENCE FOR EARLY HISTORY: the first phase of Dark Dwarf history is a
SURFACE history — a steppe people with herds. The underground identity accrued
over time rather than being present from the start.

THEY WOKE IN THE WRONG COUNTRY (confirmed, v006): their inherited craft is
forging, mining and stonework, and their terrain of origin is open, dry,
treeless high steppe. For roughly two decades a people who woke knowing how to
sink a shaft and control a quench had no ore beneath them, no fuel to burn and
no mountain to cut. They herded, and the only inherited craft the steppe
permitted was stonework. The Dark Dwarves woke somewhere that could feed them
and could not employ them. See Kingdoms_History for the founding chain that
follows from this, including the two founding dates of Hellar.

WHY THEY NEVER BECAME THE DARK ELVES: their larder stayed on the surface. The
steppe herds never stopped — they are the food base, supplemented but never
replaced by the grain, fruit and fiber of the Binding Trade. Hellar is
underground and its pantry is above it, and the daily traffic between the two
has never stopped. Dark Elves and Cave Gnomes woke underground and had no such
tether. This is why the Dark Dwarves remain the least sensitive of the
underground races and the only one that still travels and trades in person,
despite being effectively blinded by daylight without protection.
The specific herd animal of the steppe is UNDEVELOPED.

THE KULDOR POPULATION: a substantial Dark Dwarf population lives at Kuldor on
the west coast, descended from the small northern timber settlements that merged
into the Ice Dwarf port for protection. Everything past Kuldor's cliff-face
threshold is their work, and for most travellers in Lanamyr it is the only Dark
Dwarf architecture they will ever stand inside. See Geography.

TIMBER DEPENDENCY: Deep mining demands enormous quantities of worked timber —
props, shoring, ladders, hafts — and charcoal for smelting, none of which
steppe or thorn scrub provides. The Dark Dwarves exploited the boreal woodland
on their northern and eastern margins first. Boreal forest grows slowly and
recovers poorly, and stripping it is what eventually drove them to seek timber
from the Wood Elves. Because they are poor woodworkers, what they want is not
raw logs but finished Wood Elf joinery, cut and fitted. See Kingdoms_History
for the western Alios founding history and the structure of that trade.

GNOME SUBTYPES — EXTENDED ENTRIES
================================================================================

SKY GNOMES
Origin: Myst island and surrounding smaller islands. Kingdom: Myst.
Sky Gnomes are one of the most intelligent races in all of Lanamyr. This
exceptional intelligence expresses itself through invention, scholarship, and
ingenuity. Wilward Cobbersplint — a Sky Gnome who built and operated an airship
— is a natural product of this cultural trait. The Nexus on Myst, the hub of
all Jaunt Gates, is consistent with Myst as a center of knowledge and invention.
Native language: Irturi.

CAVE GNOMES
Origin: Beneath the Tarantar Mountains, northern Alios.
Cave Gnomes have an almost alien appearance among all the races of Lanamyr.
Their eyes are large and round with a pale, yellow-white color — very little
variety. Many Cave Gnomes have no hair at all; those who do have it in shades
of silver and white. Their skin is pale. They are possibly the most reclusive
race in all of Lanamyr — even more so than Dark Elves. It would be very rare
to encounter a Cave Gnome outside their own region. They do not leave their
caves often, and when they are above ground they almost always have their face
and eyes covered for protection from the sun. Encountering one in the wider
world would be a genuinely unusual event.
Native language: Omathi.

ROCK GNOMES
Origin: Western end of Coldwind Mountains, Utoa.
Accomplished gem cutters and jewelers. One of the largest diamond mines in all
of Lanamyr is located in the Coldwind Mountains where they originated. Their
skill with gemstones and precious materials has shaped their cultural identity
and their reputation across Lanamyr.
Native language: Eugali.

WOODLAND GNOMES
Origin: Southern Edura near the Iron Peaks.
The shortest of all gnome subtypes at 3'4", but their average weight is similar
to taller gnome types — the mass is simply compressed into a shorter frame.
This makes them thicker and wider than other gnomes, and physically stronger
despite their stature. Their compact powerful build makes them effective climbers.
Their skin tones are generally darker browns, sometimes with a hint of a green
hue. Hair and eyes run in darker tones as well.
Native language: Enaric.
