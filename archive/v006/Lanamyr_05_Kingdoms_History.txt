WESTERN ALIOS — FOUNDING HISTORY (2ND EPOCH, YEARS 0–221)
================================================================================

STATUS: CONFIRMED (reviewed in session v005). The sequence and dating below
are locked in as the working history of the region. Locked is not permanent —
this chain was built from three races in isolation, and as the surrounding
races are developed, conflicts will be resolved case by case rather than by
defending what is already written.

SCOPE OF THIS SECTION — READ BEFORE DRAWING CONCLUSIONS: this chain tracks
three races only. Where it is silent about contact with anyone else, that
silence is the limit of what has been developed, NOT a statement that no such
contact occurred. The Sundering map places other undeveloped races within
reach of the western arm — the Plains Elves to the east across the neck of the
continent, and the Mystic Kobolds to the south, who are in fact nearer to the
Wood Elves than the Plains Elves are. The overland corridor east toward the
central steppes is the western half of what becomes The Road in the 3rd Epoch.
Nothing here should be read as claiming that corridor went unwalked. Revisit
this section as each neighboring race is built.

The three races of the western arm of Alios: Wood Elves in the forested
lowland interior, Stone Giants in the Tempest Peaks along the northern coast,
Dark Dwarves on the dry steppe and Forbidden Peaks of the southwest. See Races
for trait values and Geography for terrain.

GEOMETRY: measured on Lanamyr_Sundering.jpg, the Stone Giant origin lies
roughly half as far from the Wood Elves as the Dark Dwarf origin does. Contact
order follows from this: Wood Elves meet Stone Giants first, Dark Dwarves
considerably later.

THE ARC AND THE BOWL (confirmed; verified against Lanamyr_Features.jpg and
Lanamyr_Biomes.jpg in session v006): the Forbidden Peaks, the Darkened Slopes
and the Tempest Peaks are not three separate features. They are one continuous
highland arc running along the outer west and north coast of the western arm,
and the Wood Elf forest sits in the bowl enclosed by it. Dark Dwarves hold the
southwest end of the arc, Stone Giants the north end, Wood Elves the interior.
The biome banding runs dry at the southwest end and grades wetter and colder
to the northeast — thorn scrub, then steppe, then boreal moist forest. See
Geography.
CONSEQUENCE — DARK DWARVES AND STONE GIANTS NEVER MEET IN THIS WINDOW. The two
races sit at opposite ends of the same range with high ground between them that
neither has any reason to cross. Giants under population pressure move DOWNHILL
into the Wood Elf lowlands or down to the coast; travelling laterally along the
arc means staying high and cold and solves nothing for them. The Dark Dwarves,
for their part, will not pay to haul over a ridge when a cheaper route exists.
This is not a near-miss but a structural non-event, and it holds until the Ice
Dwarves arrive by sea in 221 and touch both peoples in a single voyage.

THE SCATTER (years 0–20)
Thousands of adults wake scattered across each region with no memory and no
knowledge that anyone else exists. Language is intact, so the moment two
strangers meet they can speak. Gathering takes months, not generations.
Terrain determines the speed:
- DARK DWARVES consolidate fastest. On open steppe smoke is visible for miles
  and travel is unobstructed. The terrain that offers them no shelter offers
  the quickest coalescence.
- WOOD ELVES consolidate slowly. Forest limits sight and travel; bands form
  along rivers and in clearings and only gradually learn of one another. With
  High magic and little else to distinguish anyone, the earliest leaders are
  those whose ability is most visible.
- STONE GIANTS barely consolidate. Rugged ground, fewer bodies across it,
  Minimal technology to ease travel. Clumps stay small and isolated. With
  Minimal magic and Minimal craft, physical prowess is the only available axis
  of distinction, so leadership forms around strength immediately. Their
  regard for strength above all is not a value they adopted — it was the only
  thing there was to respect.

YEAR 7 — ADOMORN (confirmed lore; consequences drawn here)
Adomorn arrives at a Wood Elf people seven years into gathering: bands and
households, no kingdom and no throne. He chooses fewer than ten families and
begins the Heaven's Oak chamber.
- THE SETTLEMENT IS THE SITE OF CALDALUS (confirmed). The gathering Adomorn
  walked into stood where the capital of Faelyn would eventually rise, and
  Heaven's Oak is a central feature of the present-day city rather than a
  remote landmark. Faelyn's capital grew up around the containment chamber.
- THE CHAMBER IS ANOMALOUS AT GROUND LEVEL. In year 7 the Wood Elves build in
  the canopy and essentially nowhere else — ground construction does not arrive
  until expansion opens the first substantial clearings, later. A chamber sunk
  into the earth at the foot of a great tree is therefore unlike anything the
  Wood Elves were building at the time, and unlike anything they yet knew how
  to build. It was raised under outside direction, using knowledge that was not
  theirs, in a mode they did not practice. This is the technological island in
  its most literal form: a Drasu structure at ground level beneath a people who
  lived in the trees.
- IT ALSO PREDATES THE ETHIC, which forms in years 30 to 60. The chamber is
  not an exception to the minimal-impact philosophy; it predates the philosophy
  entirely.
- THE GUARDIAN FAMILIES ARE FOUNDING FAMILIES OF THE CAPITAL. Adomorn chose
  from one gathering settlement, not from the scattered Wood Elf people as a
  whole, so fewer than ten families represents a substantial bloc of that
  community. The Guardian lines are old-stock Caldalus families from the
  beginning — prominent, established, and entirely unremarkable in holding some
  dignified hereditary duty nobody thinks to ask about.
- THE GUARDIANS PREDATE FAELYN. Their confirmed position outside normal Wood
  Elf society structure follows directly: he founded them before that structure
  existed. There was no king to ask.
- THE GUARDIAN FAMILIES ARE A TECHNOLOGICAL ISLAND. Building a containment
  chamber under Drasu direction placed Drasu-level knowledge in Wood Elf hands
  in year 7. Those families know things no other Wood Elves know, permanently
  and in secret.
- WITHIN THIS WINDOW THE OAK IS RECENT HISTORY, NOT LEGEND. The mythology that
  eventually grows around Heaven's Oak takes several Wood Elf generations to
  settle, and a generation runs 75 to 80 years — so the folk belief described
  in Geography belongs to roughly year 250 onward, AFTER this period. Across
  the whole of years 0 to 221 there are Wood Elves alive who heard about the
  tall bronze-skinned stranger from a parent who was there. A few outliers of
  the emergence generation itself survive late into the window. The secret
  during these centuries is fresh and awkward, defended by people one or two
  removes from the event, not by an order insulated behind centuries of
  reverence. See Geography, HEAVEN'S OAK.
- CONSEQUENCE REACHING FORWARD TO THE ELF WAR. Because the chamber sits in the
  capital, the Abaculus's objective in 387–392 AW — break the Guardians and
  compromise the chamber — means the two-front assault from the Tempest Peaks
  and The Deep was converging on Caldalus itself. Diquyk was driving at the
  capital from the first day, and no one on the Wood Elf side knew why.

CONSOLIDATION (years 20–80) — THE ORIGIN OF COHESION
Lifespan and terrain together determine whether each race became one people.
- WOOD ELVES receive forty-five years of peace. Children are born from the
  first years onward — a portion of those who woke were already within the
  reproductive window of 50 to 125 — but childhood runs to 42, so the first
  Wood Elf born after the Sundering does not reach adulthood until roughly
  year 45. What is flat for that entire span is the ADULT population: no new
  claimants to land, no new contenders for leadership, no new households
  competing for the same ground. Land is abundant against a static adult
  population and there is nothing to fight over, so they sort themselves out
  by negotiation. High cohesion is purchased by their lifespan.
- STONE GIANTS receive nine. Adult at 8–12 and reproducing from 10, their
  numbers climb within the first decade and never stop. By the time
  consolidation might have occurred, rival holdings are entrenched with twenty
  years of feud behind them, and leadership by strength means every claim is
  contestable. Low cohesion is locked in by roughly year 50 and never comes
  unlocked.
- DARK DWARVES fall between and come out ahead. Adults at 26, giving a
  generation of static population — long enough to consolidate before pressure
  arrives, and they were already gathering fastest. High cohesion, and the
  first of the three to function as a single people.

HELLAR — THE FOUNDING (years 0–150)

THEY WOKE IN THE WRONG COUNTRY. The Dark Dwarf technology baseline is skilled
forging, expert mining and fine stonework, with weak woodworking. Their terrain
of origin is open, dry, treeless high steppe. For the first two decades a people
who woke knowing how to sink a shaft and control a quench had no ore beneath
them, no fuel to burn and no mountain to cut. They herded. The only inherited
craft the steppe permitted was stonework, and they used it the one way treeless
high country allows — dry-stone steadings, windbreaks and stock pens. This is
the exact inverse of the Wood Elves, who woke in forest and have never lived
anywhere else. The Dark Dwarves woke somewhere that could feed them and could
not employ them.

UNITY BEFORE A CAPITAL (years 0–26). The steppe is legible: smoke carries,
travel is unobstructed, and nothing can be massed or built out of sight. No
faction could form in secret, so none did. Dark Dwarf unity was never won,
because the ground gave no one anywhere to contest it from.
The consequence is that they were ONE PEOPLE ON OPEN GROUND for a full
generation before they had a city to be one people in — the reverse of the usual
order. Hellar is the seat of a nation that already existed, not the thing that
made one. Their founding institution is a moot of band heads convened on the
steppe, and when the city was finally cut, the moot simply moved underground.
When pressure arrives at year 26 — the first Sundering-born generation reaching
adulthood, the adult population climbing for the first time — it lands on a
people already unified. This is the whole difference from the Stone Giants.
Giant pressure fractured a people who had never cohered; Dark Dwarf pressure was
handed to an institution that already existed, and became a thing to be
administered rather than fought over.

THE TWO DIRECTIONS. What the steppe lacked lay at opposite ends of their own
arc. Northeast up the Darkened Slopes: boreal moist forest, timber and fuel.
Southwest in the Forbidden Peaks: thorn scrub above, deep stone and ore below.
They went both ways, and the steppe between became a haul corridor. The Dark
Dwarves spent the better part of a century moving material up and down their own
territory before they ever met another race. THIS IS THE ORIGIN of their
transactional disposition and of their being the only western Alios race that
travels TO others — they were organized around a long haul before there was
anyone at the far end of it.
The seat went south because a working is not a place you visit. Timber can be
hauled; a shaft cannot. So the city was cut where the ore was, and the forest
was worked at the end of a supply line.

THE TWO FOUNDING DATES (confirmed, and contested in-world):
  YEAR 41 — the first deep shaft is sunk in the northern Forbidden Peaks.
  YEAR 54 — the first great hall is cut and the moot convenes underground for
    the first time. The name Hellar attaches from this point.
Both dates are kept exactly, and which one founded Hellar has been argued for
twenty-four centuries. THE ARGUMENT IS A CONSTITUTIONAL DISPUTE WEARING A
COSTUME: year 41 is the crown's date and year 54 is the moot's, and each side's
date is its own claim to primacy. A people who perfect rather than invent keep
their records precisely and disagree about what the records MEAN rather than
about what they say.

WHAT HELLAR IS. Cut, not built. Fine stonework is a baseline skill, so the
earliest galleries are already accomplished masonry. WHAT IMPROVED ACROSS THE
CENTURIES WAS DEPTH AND SCALE, NEVER QUALITY — which is what "perfecters rather
than inventors" looks like on the ground. The work was good on the first day and
only ever got bigger.

GOVERNANCE — TWO AUTHORITIES, ONE ABOVE AND ONE BELOW (TENTATIVE):
  THE MOOT decides what the people do. It predates the city by decades and has
    never conceded that anything outranks it.
  A WORKING is directed absolutely by whoever holds it. A deep shaft takes
    decades, costs enormously, and kills people if directed by committee, so
    underground there is one voice and no appeal.
Kingship grows out of the second. The holder of the year-41 shaft directed what
became the city's central axis, and directing the axis became directing the
city. HELLAR'S CROWN IS ATTACHED TO A HOLE IN THE GROUND. The Moot never
dissolved, which is why Hellar is a kingdom with a standing assembly older than
its throne. Tecali names for the Moot and for a working, the etymology of
"Hellar," and the name of its first king are all UNDEVELOPED.

THE TIMBER TRAP (years ~60–150). Deep mining eats worked timber — props,
shoring, ladders, hafts — and smelting eats charcoal faster. The Dark Dwarves
are weak woodworkers who treat wood as material to acquire rather than craft to
practice, so they took the boreal margins the only way they knew: fast, at
volume, with no replanting, on forest that recovers badly.
THE FRONTIER TURNED EAST, NOT NORTH. Of the two timber directions, north up the
Darkened Slopes is high, cold and exposed, with the haul running uphill along a
ridge; east and inland is lower, warmer, shorter to good stands, and the hauling
runs downhill. The weight of the expansion therefore went east and compounded
for roughly a century. The northern slope settlements exist but stay SMALL, SLOW
AND MARGINAL — held because the stands are there rather than because the ground
is good — and never push far enough up the arc to matter. This is why the
Darkened Slopes read as occupied to an arriving fleet in 221 and still amount to
very little, and it is why the Dark Dwarves met the Wood Elves rather than the
Stone Giants. They did not wander into the elves. They followed the cheapest
haul available to them, and the cheapest haul pointed straight at Faelyn's dry
southern margin. See SECOND CONTACT below.

FIRST CONTACT — THE STONE GIANTS (years ~40–60)
Geometry places the Giants nearest; demography puts them in motion. They have
been growing for thirty years while the Wood Elves have not grown at all. They
come downhill.
Unlike contact within a race, this encounter carries a language barrier and the
first exchanges are mutually incomprehensible.
THE PERMANENT STALEMATE: the Wood Elves answer with magic, which the Giants
have never seen and cannot match. High magic against Minimal magic and Minimal
technology. The Giants outweigh the elves and can take ground but cannot hold
it against magic; the elves can repel anything that comes down but cannot
pursue into the peaks against that size on that terrain. Neither side can ever
finish the other.
THE RAID CYCLE: Giant population booms against the carrying capacity of the
boreal rain forest every few generations. Pressure has one direction to go —
downhill into the elf lowlands. The elves push it back, losses reset the
population, and it builds again. This repeats for the remainder of the epoch
and beyond. It requires no villain and no grievance; the demography produces it
on a schedule the elves can nearly predict and can never negotiate away,
because a Giant chief's word dies with him inside twenty years and Low cohesion
leaves no institution to outlast him.
THE CANOPY AS DEFENSE: Wood Elf canopy construction is extraordinarily
effective against Giants, whose size becomes a liability in a network of
walkways built for smaller frames. They can smash and burn at ground level and
cannot reach what is above it, and there is no wall to break because the
structure is distributed across hundreds of trees.
BEARING ON THE ELF WAR: this is why Diquyk's recruitment of the Stone Giants in
383–387 AW worked. He was the first force in their history capable of
neutralizing the elves' only advantage — offering not plunder so much as the
end of a stalemate their ancestors had lost for a hundred generations. The
plunder was the terms, not the reason.

SECOND CONTACT — THE DARK DWARVES (years ~100–150)
The Dark Dwarves come north and east looking for wood, having stripped the
slow-growing boreal woodland on their own margins. They reach the Wood Elves at
the dry southern edge of the forest.
They arrive in the opposite mode to the Giants: with a proposition and
something to offer. First contact is a negotiation rather than a collision.
Within a century the Wood Elves therefore learn two incompatible lessons about
outsiders — that strangers can be something to survive, and that strangers can
be something worth having. This is the origin of their guarded-but-integrative
disposition.

THE BINDING TRADE (years ~150–221)
Real ore reaches the Wood Elves for the first time. Bog iron was all they had
ever had, and Low ingenuity against poor material is why a century and a half
produced only simple forging.
WHAT MOVES, AND WHAT DOES NOT:
  Elves to Dwarves — worked timber: props, shoring, ladders, hafts, joinery.
    Agricultural surplus: grain, fruit, fiber, which the steppe does not
    provide. NOT bulk charcoal.
  Dwarves to Elves — finished tools and implements, the bulk of the trade.
    Cut stone and stonework. Raw ore only in limited quantity.
THE PERMANENT FRICTION: smelting is the largest consumer of wood of any craft,
and the Wood Elves will not sell fuel. This caps Dark Dwarf smelting at
whatever their own depleted woodland supports, leaving them sitting on more ore
than they can ever process beside an effectively limitless forest whose owners
will not sell them the one thing they need. Magic offers them no escape:
inefficiency scales badly and their need is industrial. The friction is
permanent, structural, and contains no villain — the two peoples simply value a
tree differently.
WHY THE ELVES PREFER FINISHED GOODS: raw ore does not escape their problem, it
imports it. Smelting ore themselves would require making charcoal, which means
cutting. Finished tools cost them nothing, because the burning already happened
on dwarf fuel. The consequence is that they remain dependent on dwarf craft
permanently and never develop metallurgy of their own.
Over this same period the Dark Dwarves go deeper and spend less time above
ground. The long adaptation of their eyes begins here.
WHY IT BEGINS NOW AND NOT EARLIER: through the first century they were a surface
people with a mine, and their own forest capped how deep they could go. Once the
Binding Trade secured finished joinery from Faelyn they could sink deeper than
their own woodland had ever permitted, and the proportion of a life spent under
stone finally crossed over. The latent predisposition had nothing to express
itself against until there were generations who scarcely went up.
THE TRADE IS WHAT TOOK THEIR EYES. The timber that let them go deeper is what
cost them their sight, and neither people ever knew it.
WHY THEY NEVER BECAME THE DARK ELVES: their larder stayed on the surface. The
steppe herds never stopped — they are the food base, and the Binding Trade only
ever supplemented them with the grain, fruit and fiber the steppe cannot
provide. Hellar is underground and its pantry is above it, and the daily traffic
between the two has never stopped. Dark Elves and Cave Gnomes woke underground
and had no such tether. This is the origin of the confirmed present-day trait:
the Dark Dwarves remain the least sensitive of the underground races and the
only one that still travels and trades in person, because they have been walking
up out of their own city every day for twenty-four centuries.

YEAR 221 — THE ICE DWARVES (confirmed lore; sequence expanded in v006)
An Ice Dwarf expedition from Icefall sails south, finds the Tempest Peaks and
the Darkened Slopes already occupied, and establishes trade routes that build
friendly relations with the neighboring peoples over time.
They arrive BY SEA, which is the significant detail — the region's first
contact from beyond the continent, and its first arrival by fleet. This is a
narrower claim than it may appear: it does NOT mean the western arm had met no
one before 221. See the SCOPE note at the head of this section. What is new in
221 is the direction and the means, not the fact of outsiders.

THE SEQUENCE (confirmed):
1. Sailing south from Icefall, the fleet makes landfall at the northern end of
   the arc, on the edges of Stone Giant territory in the Tempest Peaks.
2. The encounters are TENSE and any peace is short-lived. This is not a choice
   so much as arithmetic: Stone Giant cohesion is Low, a chief's word dies with
   him inside twenty years, and there is no institution capable of holding an
   agreement. The Ice Dwarves decline to establish a settlement there.
3. The fleet continues SOUTH AND WEST along the outer coast — following the arc
   down past the Tempest Peaks toward the Darkened Slopes — and eventually finds
   unoccupied ground. This gap exists because the Dark Dwarf northern slope
   settlements are small and marginal and never pushed far up the arc.
4. They settle, and before long meet the Dark Dwarves. Relations go far better
   than they did with the Giants. The settlement becomes KULDOR — see Geography.
CONSEQUENCE — WHICH NEIGHBORS BECAME FRIENDLY: the friendly relations that the
confirmed lore says these routes built over time are with the DARK DWARVES and
in due course the WOOD ELVES. They are not with the Stone Giants.
CONSEQUENCE — THE GIANTS AND ICE DWARF METAL: the arrival still offers the
Giants a source of worked metal that does not come through the Wood Elves, and
it is the FIRST OFFER EVER MADE to them, since Hellar never traded metal to the
Giants and the two peoples never met. What the Giants do with it is UNDEVELOPED,
and whether their Low cohesion even permits a sustained trading relationship is
an open question.

THE KINSHIP IS PRACTICAL, NOT SENTIMENTAL. Shared Drasu heritage produces
nothing by itself — the Ice Dwarves and Iron Dwarves share it and have no strong
direct ties, trading only through intermediaries. If the Ice and Dark Dwarves
become close it is because contact history and complementary craft made them so.
This also explains the coldness toward Fardar: Icefall met the Dark Dwarves in
221 and built on it for millennia, and never had that with the Iron Dwarves.
A TENSION WORTH HOLDING: Icefall is Polar Ice and has no timber either. Two
mining peoples arriving at the same forest is not obviously kinship, and whether
Ice Dwarf sea haul eventually relieved the Dark Dwarves' timber problem or
priced them out of their own trade is UNDEVELOPED. What it cannot do is break
the fuel deadlock — the Wood Elves' refusal to sell charcoal is not a transport
problem.

THE NORTHERN EXPEDITION AND NUSUL: a separate fleet departing Icefall in the
same year sails in a different direction and discovers the Tarantar Mountains
and their mineral wealth. The two ventures are INDEPENDENT and remain so — the
southern settlement plays no part in the discovery of the Tarantars. The
northern fleet sends word back to Icefall; Icefall deliberates, provisions and
commits, and a contingent sails to found Nusul in 239. This accounts for the
eighteen-year gap between discovery and founding. See NUSUL below.
THE ASYMMETRY: Icefall loses its northern venture entirely in 268 and keeps its
southern one for the next twenty-four centuries. After Nusul falls, Kuldor is
Icefall's only foreign holding, and a people who had just lost an entire kingdom
would hold the surviving one very differently.

OPEN WITHIN THIS CHAIN (deliberately not decided):
- The exact year of first Giant contact.
- Whether that first meeting turns violent immediately or passes through a
  brief confused peace.
- Whether the Stone Giants ever take Ice Dwarf metal in quantity.

NUSUL — THE LOST KINGDOM
================================================================================

OVERVIEW:
Nusul was an Ice Dwarf kingdom established in the Tarantar Mountains of northern
Alios during the 2nd Epoch. It was abandoned after 29 years following a
catastrophic encounter with subterranean Creatures of Olum. The name Nusul is
known to the Ice Dwarves and preserved in their records and oral history but is
largely unknown to the wider world, which refers to it only as the lost kingdom.
Many adventurers have tried and failed to find it over the centuries.

By 432 AW the ruins have been abandoned for approximately 2,329 years. The
Abaculus was removed from the Tarantar Mountains in year 2136, 2nd Epoch,
after which the Olum gradually dispersed. The ruins have been sitting undisturbed
and increasingly quiet for approximately 461 years.

THE OLUM CONNECTION:
The Abaculus was hidden in the Tarantar Mountains from year 7 of the 2nd Epoch
onward. Creatures of Olum had been expanding outward from that central location
for 232 years before the Ice Dwarves arrived. The Ice Dwarves never knew the
true reason for the Olum presence — to them the mountains were simply
inexplicably and relentlessly dangerous, overrun with Creatures in numbers that
made no natural sense.

CONTENTS:
The kingdom was unfinished at the time of abandonment. Substantial infrastructure
had been carved into the mountain over 29 years. The ruins contain:
- Locally forged tools, weapons, and structural ironwork produced from Tarantar
  ore using conventional forging techniques. The conditions for Ice Forging did
  not exist in the Tarantar Mountains — smiths there worked with the local ore
  using standard methods. The Tarantar ore was high quality in its own right.
- Genuine Ice Forged weapons and tools that arrived via supply runs from Icefall
  before the fall. Given the remarkable durability of Ice Forged blades and
  the cold mountain environment, these could be in extraordinary condition after
  2,300+ years.
- Architecture, chambers, and mine workings abandoned mid-construction.
- The mine that punched through into the Olum cavern in year 268.

THE LEGEND:
As the story filtered through trade routes and intermediaries over centuries,
details were lost and distorted. Multiple versions circulate — a giant dragon
seeking mineral wealth, Dark Elf treachery, and other explanations have all
been attributed to the kingdom's downfall. The truth — subterranean Creatures
of Olum — is known only to the Ice Dwarves. The Dark Elf version is considered
plausible by many, given that Dark Elves occupy The Deep beneath the Tarantar
Mountains and are geographically the closest neighbors the kingdom had.

NUSUL TIMELINE (2nd Epoch):
Year 7:   Abaculus hidden in Tarantar Mountains. Creatures of Olum begin
          expanding outward from that location over the following centuries.
Year 221: First Ice Dwarf expeditions depart Icefall in multiple directions.
          One fleet discovers the Tarantar Mountains and their mineral wealth
          and sends word back to Icefall. This fleet is INDEPENDENT of the
          southern expedition that founds Kuldor in the same year; the two
          ventures never informed one another.
221-239:  Icefall deliberates, provisions and commits. The eighteen-year gap
          between discovery and founding is this decision and its preparation.
Year 239: Kingdom location established. Named Nusul. Regular supply runs and
          labor forces begin. Olum have been expanding in the mountains for
          232 years by this point.
Year 253: First major Olum encounter. Dwarves fight them off and continue
          working. Valuable mineral deposits found as they dig deeper. Attacks
          grow more frequent as the population grows.
Year 261: Major defeat. Part of the mines closed off. Reinforcements sent from
          Icefall. Permanent guards and scouts deployed. Expansion attempts
          consistently end in being pushed back.
Year 268: A mine punches through into a large subterranean cavern swarming
          with Olum. The kingdom is overrun and lost. All inhabitants killed.
          Later that same year, the last supply ship arrives from Icefall,
          finds silence and Creatures of Olum, and immediately returns to
          Icefall with the news. The Ice Dwarves never return.

3RD EPOCH KINGDOMS (432 AW)
================================================================================

ALIOS:
Kimora: Central Alios. Hillfolk humans. Capital: Shatazaar.
  Borders: South — Ash Mountains; East — Black Mountains of Fardar;
  West — natural border line of hills and mountains (includes Monowi Hills)
  running from Ash Mountains to Tarantar Mountains; North — Tarantar Mountains
  east to coast; Northeast — low plains bounded by Wandering Hills.
  Ruler: King Gralvin Sutton (The Reluctant King).
  Contains ruins of Rohl and the Burnt Plains.
Fardar: Black Mountains, central Alios. Iron Dwarves. One of the richest
  kingdoms in Lanamyr. Relations with Kimora strained after Tarik's crimes.
  Territory encompasses the western and central bulk of the Black Mountains.
  The far eastern end of the range is Tunneler Kobold territory — within
  Fardar's borders but distant enough to avoid significant conflict.
Ellendale: Central-west Alios. Plains Elves. Played a key role in overthrow
  of Tarik alongside the Herringers. Ruler undeveloped.
Faelyn: Western arm of Alios. Wood Elves. Capital: Caldalus.
  Home of Guardians of the Tree. Mya Li is from Faelyn.
Hellar: Underground city in Forbidden Peaks, western arm of Alios. Dark Dwarves.
  Effectively blinded by daylight without protection. Founded years 41/54 of the
  2nd Epoch — see WESTERN ALIOS founding history above. Governed by a moot older
  than its crown.
Kuldor: Port city on the west coast of the western arm, at the foot of the
  Darkened Slopes. NOT A KINGDOM — founded by Ice Dwarves in 221 of the 2nd
  Epoch and later merged with the nearby Dark Dwarf settlements. One of the
  largest port cities and trading hubs in Alios and the western terminus of the
  great east-west road. Mostly Ice and Dark Dwarves, with sizeable Sky Gnome,
  human and Wood Elf populations. Governance UNDEVELOPED — it would be the first
  polity in Lanamyr that is not a race-kingdom. See Geography.
Andar: Large northern peninsula of Alios. Northmen humans. Ruler undeveloped.

EDURA:
T'sarra: Northern Edura. High Elves. Home of the Var Harma. Tropical Rain Forest
  biome — lush and warm. Contains the ruins of Atral and the Drasian Vault.
  Ruler and internal politics undeveloped.
  A CLOSED AND ANTAGONISTIC KINGDOM: T'sarra's reclusiveness is a posture rather
  than a temperament. The High Elves hold themselves superior to every other
  race, and their dealings with other kingdoms and civilizations are
  transactional throughout — every arrangement measured for leverage or
  advantage. They are not a hostile military power in the open. They are a
  closed state that works through acquisition, information, and the quiet
  destabilization of rivals, largely via the external branch of the Var Harma.
  And they sit atop what may be the largest single source of undiscovered
  knowledge and power in all of Lanamyr, the Abaculus excepted.
  THE CITIZENRY: Ordinary High Elves of T'sarra are raised on a curated account
  of their own kingdom — that all is well, that T'sarra is the envy of the
  world, and that the races beyond its borders are lesser. Contact with the
  outside is controlled and the true shape of the kingdom's dealings is held by
  a narrow few. Most citizens are not complicit so much as convinced. This
  leaves ample room for sympathetic High Elves who have simply never been given
  anything to weigh their upbringing against.
  Individuals who leave and see the wider world firsthand are the kingdom's
  central vulnerability — they are the only ones positioned to discover that the
  account they were raised on is false. See THE NORTHWARD ENVOYS above.
  HIGH ELVES ABROAD: Because T'sarra is closed, High Elves are the exception to
  the general mingling and spread of the races across Lanamyr through the 2nd
  and 3rd Epochs. A High Elf encountered outside T'sarra in 432 AW is far more
  likely to be a defector, a long-settled descendant of one, or an agent of the
  Var Harma than a citizen abroad on ordinary business.

GUARDIANS OF THE TREE
Ancient hereditary Wood Elf secret order from Faelyn. Protect Heaven's Oak and
the Abaculus containment chamber. Founded early 2nd Epoch by Adomorn — fewer
than 10 founding Wood Elf families chosen personally. Answer only to Adomorn.
Outside normal Wood Elf society structure. Aware of Abaculus and chamber's
three purposes. May mate with non-Guardian pure Wood Elves; no non-Wood Elf
mixing allowed. Still vigilant against Elf War threat; unaware strategy shifted
to Greyhawk resurrection. Founding family names: UNDEVELOPED.

THE ORIGINAL MEMBERS (confirmed): the first Guardians were the HEADS of each
family Adomorn chose — not the families entire. The order has always been a
small number of individuals drawn from a small number of lines.

SUCCESSION (confirmed): the role and its purpose pass by blood, to ONE
descendant per family per generation, typically the firstborn, who is sworn to
secrecy on being brought in. This is what keeps the order small enough to hold
a secret across two and a half millennia. Were every descendant of the founding
families to inherit, the order would number in the thousands by the present day
and could not possibly have stayed concealed.

TENTATIVE — TIMING OF THE OATH: the heir is sworn in upon reaching adulthood,
with the serving Guardian continuing in the role. NOT fully confirmed. Under
this model the order holds roughly twice its nominal number at any moment —
serving Guardians plus sworn heirs who may wait a century or more, since a
Guardian parent reproducing between 50 and 125 may live to 250. The advantages
are that the knowledge is never one death away from being lost, and that a long
apprenticeship under a serving parent is possible.

ANOINTMENT FROM OUTSIDE THE BLOODLINES (confirmed that it has occurred;
instances UNDEVELOPED): Guardians have on occasion been anointed from outside
the founding lines. TENTATIVE — one plausible cause is a Guardian being
murdered for some nefarious purpose, breaking a line and forcing the order to
look beyond blood.

STRUCTURAL WEAKNESS — THE IDENTIFIABLE HEIR: firstborn succession means the
next Guardian of every line is publicly identifiable long before they are
sworn, and in the years before the oath they do not know themselves. Anyone who
worked out what the order was could simply count firstborns. This is a
deliberate vulnerability in the design, not an oversight.

TENTATIVE — BETRAYAL OF THE OATH: a sworn Guardian breaking secrecy is an
available story thread. Nothing established.

THE MARAUDERS
Elite Wood Elf unit formed in 392 AW for the final incursion into Dark Elf
territory — formed AFTER Mya had already experienced her crushing first defeat
against Diquyk's forces. They are NOT the unit present in the first encounter
with Diquyk's forces.
Mya's first love interest (Kharis Ailwin) was killed in that earlier encounter,
along with several other men under her command.

FORMATION AND ORIGIN (confirmed):
After the first major encounter, Mya returned to Faelyn accompanied by Adomorn.
A council of war followed — King Aer'Raenal, Adomorn, Mya, and a number of
Faelyn's tacticians and strategists. The names and number of those advisors are
undeveloped and not presently important.
Until this point, Diquyk's two-front campaign had kept Faelyn's forces entirely
on the defensive. The council concluded that the best remaining chance was to
abandon that posture and go on the offensive — to strike into Dark Elf
territory rather than continue absorbing attacks.
Mya volunteered to lead the attack.
She did NOT hand-pick the unit. She went to her peers and fellow soldiers and
told them plainly what she intended: the mission would be dangerous and very
likely end in death, but she believed in her heart it was the best chance to
save Faelyn, and she was prepared to fight to the death. Anyone who wished to
join would be welcome. Anyone who chose to stay behind would carry no blame and
no ill will from her.
Ten came forward. All ten were volunteers who fully understood it was likely a
suicide mission. Their love of kingdom, family, and friends was stronger than
their fear and hesitation.

TOTAL STRENGTH: 10 Marauders.
FINAL BATTLE STRUCTURE: The Marauders were divided for the final incursion into
The Deep. 8 accompanied Mya Li as the deliberate decoy force — entering The
Deep through conventional means with the intent of drawing Diquyk's attention
and engagement. 2 accompanied Adomorn through a Jaunt Gate located inside The
Deep, deep within Dark Elf territory, to flank from behind. Adomorn put the
2 Marauders to sleep for the gate transit. How the Marauders entered The Deep
conventionally and how the sleep was timed or lifted are undeveloped.
Diquyk sensed the magical presence of the gate transit — his ability to detect
others drawing on ambient energy extended to the significant magical activity
of gate use — and was tipped off to Adomorn's approach, foiling the ambush.
Despite this, Diquyk was still engaged on two fronts. He intercepted Adomorn's
spells and redirected them as blue flame, killing multiple Marauders. When Mya
and Adomorn switched to physical combat, cutting off Diquyk's fuel supply, a
barrier went up around him — raised at the last instant and close to reflexive,
the body's answer to a beating that had nearly killed him. Adomorn tore open a
doorway into the banishment plane. Diquyk's ability to intercept a casting never
depended on what he had stored; it was simply his. What failed him was the
physical state he was in. He did not react in time, and the two of them pushed
him through the opening still sealed inside his own shell. Adomorn closed it
behind him. All 10 Marauders were dead by battle's end. Only Mya and
Adomorn survived — though see tentative note in Adomorn's entry (Characters
file) regarding his possible death immediately after the banishment.
Individual member names and backgrounds: UNDEVELOPED.
STRATEGIC MOTIVATION FOR THE ELF WAR: The Abaculus learned of Heaven's Oak
and the Guardians of the Tree through Greyhawk's memories at the moment of his
corruption. The chamber at Heaven's Oak — built by Adomorn as a permanent
containment solution — represented a direct threat to the Abaculus's freedom.
The Abaculus directed Diquyk to wage war on Faelyn to break the Guardians and
compromise the chamber before he pursued the larger mission.

THE VAR HARMA (THE CURATORS)
High Elf secretive society from T'sarra. Founded by royal decree. Operates on a
need-to-know basis internally — lower ranking members are not privy to the
organization's deepest knowledge or connections. Known member: Maeglin
Leralonde, renowned within the organization as a thief and dungeon crawler.

ORIGIN: The High Elves emerged in T'sarra north of the ruins of Atral. T'sarra
was established first. Some period later — length UNDECIDED and deliberately
left open — they discovered the ruins and began excavating. The region was
contested by Mountain Trolls coming down from the Troll's Breath Mountains. The
Sand Dwarves, expanding from the Void Sands southeast of T'sarra, eventually
entered the region and allied with the High Elves against the Trolls, pushing
them back.
The Var Harma were created by order of the ruler of T'sarra at that time — name
and identity UNDEVELOPED — who laid out their original purpose in that decree.
They were never a guild that grew up around the dig of its own accord. They were
chartered, and their mandate was to excavate, preserve, and protect what Atral
contained.
That founding document presumably still exists somewhere in T'sarra. It
describes an organization that no longer entirely resembles the one bearing its
name.

THE EXPANSION OF THE MANDATE: What came out of the ruins pointed to a lost
civilization of enormous scale and power. T'sarra concluded that if one such
civilization had existed and been forgotten, others might too — along with their
artifacts. The Var Harma's role expanded accordingly, from archaeology and
curation into secretive operation abroad.
Over time the organization learned that power takes many forms. Artifacts are
only the most obvious. Information has value of its own, and so does leverage
over a rival kingdom, and so does that kingdom's instability. Acquisition by
theft, and destabilization as an end in itself, both fall within what the Var
Harma now do. Whether any ruler of T'sarra ever formally authorized this
expansion, or whether successive thrones simply stopped asking, is UNDEVELOPED.

TWO BRANCHES: The organization is effectively split in two.
- THE INTERNAL BRANCH, within T'sarra's borders, still fills the original role:
  archaeologists, curators, translators, keepers of what has been recovered.
  These members largely understand the Var Harma as the founding decree
  describes it, and many are sincere in that belief.
- THE EXTERNAL BRANCH operates beyond T'sarra's borders. Spies, assassins,
  thieves, and treasure hunters make up the majority of Var Harma encountered
  outside the kingdom. They acquire artifacts of power by whatever means are
  available, gather information, and work against the stability of other
  kingdoms where it serves T'sarra.
Moving from the internal branch to the external one is not a transfer of duty
but an initiation — a member is approached, told what the organization actually
does, and asked. This is the principal reason the internal need-to-know
structure exists. How much of the external branch's work the internal branch
suspects is UNDEVELOPED.

THE DRASIAN TONGUE: Centuries of excavation have uncovered a great volume of
Drasian writing. The language itself was silenced at the Sundering and no living
race speaks it, but its traits survive beneath many present-day languages — see
the Drasu entry in the Races file. Holding the daughter tongues side by side
against the written original, the Var Harma have pieced together a partial
working knowledge of Drasian. Their reconstruction is incomplete. How much they
can read, and what else they have read, is UNDEVELOPED.
Among the writings they deciphered, the Draak were mentioned.

THE NORTHWARD ENVOYS: On the strength of those mentions, the Var Harma have
over the years sent envoys north into Alios to find the fallen civilization of
the Draak. Envoys are instructed not to divulge their purpose to anyone they
meet.
Whether any succeeded is UNDEVELOPED. A significant number never reported back.
Some of those saw how the rest of the world actually operates, understood that
what they had been taught in T'sarra was false, and cut ties — defecting rather
than returning. Others simply preferred the lands they found and settled there,
away from T'sarra's politics and influence. A fair number remain loyal and still
operate with T'sarra's interests in mind. No specific envoys, names, or actions
have been established.
Maeglin Leralonde is the most recent of these envoys. See Characters & Items.

HEADQUARTERS: Rumored to be in the ruins of Atral, directly above the Drasian
Vault. The Var Harma have been excavating Atral for centuries.

THE DRASIAN VAULT: The Vault has never been opened and cannot be opened by the
Var Harma. It admits only a Drasu. Whether they have located it by 432 AW is
undecided; finding it and entering it are separate problems, and only the second
one matters. See Geography.
NO CONNECTION TO ADOMORN: The Var Harma have no relationship with Adomorn. He is
aware of their excavations at Atral and is untroubled by them, because he knows
the Vault will not open for them. Any earlier lore suggesting he had contact
with their highest ranks is superseded.

THE HERRINGERS
Defined not by blood but by common cause and belief. Originally an Iron Dwarf
family of great wealth. Matron Arrama Herringer used family resources to resist
Bourbac's enslavement of Iron Dwarves. Organized resistance under political
protection of Lord Yllyndel the Ancient (Plains Elves). Believed to have
funded Evander's assassination of Bourbac. After Evander freed the Dwarves:
protection vanished; Arrama killed by bandits near Ash Mountains foothills.
Rekindled as Tarik's reign took hold. Grimmon Herringer (born 372 AW, died
429 AW) rebuilt the name, personally organized the coordinated overthrow, told
Mya Li firsthand stories of Tarik's atrocities. Was present at the overthrow
at age 41 (413 AW). Founded Herrington. Grimmon killed by Orc raiders in 429 AW.
Leadership and future: UNDECIDED. Grimmon did not officially name a successor.
TENTATIVE — succession would likely come down to a vote or group decision among
the higher-ranking Herringers, who would elect Qenwick Orcsbane. See Characters
& Items. Note this makes the internal structure more load-bearing, since a
voting body implies defined ranks and some means of convening a scattered
membership.
TENTATIVE STRUCTURE — A LOOSE, INFORMAL ALLIANCE: Not a formal society. There
are no membership rolls, no initiation, and nothing to join. A person is a
Herringer because they did the work — gave money, hid someone, carried a
message, opened a door. Trust moves by personal vouching rather than by
credentials, and most members know only their own link in the chain rather than
the shape of the whole.
Organized in cells, each with a local leader. This suits a movement that has
twice survived by dispersing and going dormant: there is no center to destroy,
and a cell that is lost does not expose the others.
TENTATIVE FUNCTION — FUNDERS AND ORGANIZERS, NOT SOLDIERS: Their recorded
history is one of money, coordination, and safe passage rather than direct
force. Arrama used family resources to resist and organized under Lord
Yllyndel's political protection; the Herringers are believed to have FUNDED
Evander's assassination of Bourbac rather than carrying it out. Grimmon
ORGANIZED the coordinated overthrow of Tarik and drew up the strike plans with
the Plains Elves, while Mya Li and the militias did the fighting. Twice now the
Herringers have been the hand behind the work rather than the hand doing it.
This is a large part of why membership was never written down. There was never
a roll to keep.
TENTATIVE — THE CONVOCATION: While traveling to spread word of Grimmon's death,
Qenwick met with many of these local cell leaders. Alongside the news, he would
have told them to gather at an appointed place and time to decide the fate of
the organization. The circuit and the summons are the same journey.
  Two problems this creates, both deliberate:
  - The man who calls the gathering and is then elected at it has arranged his
    own election, whether or not he intended to. He set the time, the place, and
    the terms, and everyone present came because he asked. This is visible to
    anyone who wants to see it.
  - A cell structure exists to prevent every leader being in one room.
    Convening them is the most exposed thing a resistance can do, and Grimmon
    was just killed by Orc raiders acting as instruments of the Kimora framing
    operation. Someone was already hunting Herringers.
  CONFIRMED: Bor, Maeglin, and Skitter — who made that circuit with Qenwick —
  were aware of what he was doing. The four were close, and he called the
  convocation openly rather than in rooms they weren't in.
NOTE — THE KINGDOM DREAM IS NOT A HERRINGER AMBITION: The dream of a new
Dwarven kingdom in the Tarantar Mountains belongs to Grimmon and Bor personally
and is rooted in their Dwarven heritage, not in the Herringer cause. The
Herringers are a resistance movement defined by common cause and belief; the
kingdom is a separate thread that happened to live in the same man. See Grimmon
Herringer and Bor Glitterbeard in Characters & Items.

TIMELINE

--- 1ST EPOCH ---
Year 0:    Creation of Lanamyr. N'du Irridae are primary architects; some Itule
           participate. Built in secret from the Onwu. Energy harvested from
           lifeless planes of existence to fuel the world's creation — those
           planes wiped clean in the process. All life on Lanamyr created by
           the N'du and Itule — from the Drasu and Draak down to the smallest
           creature. The Drasu and Draak required the combined effort of many
           Irridae and were given intelligence and greater power. Lesser life
           was created by individual Irridae with comparatively little effort.
           Drasu and Draak civilizations established. Good and evil potential
           exists within both races.
Year 70:   N'du depart. Abaculus hidden in Black Mountains of Fardar (heart
           of Draak Territory) as a temporary measure while a permanent
           solution is sought. Draak agree to actively monitor and protect it.
           Adomorn leads this operation. The Onwu, having discovered Lanamyr
           after its creation, bound themselves to the plane and began searching
           for the Abaculus — the reason all Irridae eventually departed.
Year 245:  City of Atral founded on Edura. Drasian Vault built underground.
Year 1181: Onwu Irridae arrive at Atral. Olum creates Creatures of Olum.
Year 1334: Ah'Tabai returns to Lanamyr to prepare for the Onwu threat. In an
           act of poor judgement, retrieves the Abaculus from the Black
           Mountains intending to weaponize it against the Onwu. Corrupted on
           contact. The Abaculus is NOT in the Black Mountains when Atral falls.
           The Abaculus deliberately prolongs Ah'Tabai's corruption to use him
           as a tool. Ah'Tabai — fighting the corruption with the full force of
           his cosmic nature — uses his remaining time to grant Adomorn
           immortality before being fully claimed. Ah'Tabai then destroys all
           Irridae present at Atral. The sudden catastrophic annihilation of
           the Irridae resonates through their creation — the Sundering.
           Draak and Drasu shattered into all modern races simultaneously across
           all of Lanamyr, even those not present at Atral. End of 1st Epoch.
           All Irridae who had bound themselves to Lanamyr are destroyed.
           No Irridae remain in the world from this point forward.

--- 2ND EPOCH ---
Year 0:    The races wake scattered across their home regions — adults only,
           no memory, language and reasoning intact. Approximately 5,000
           individuals of each Drasu-descended race and 300 of each
           Draak-descended race (see Overview_Cosmology). Bands begin forming
           within months. WESTERN ALIOS: Dark Dwarves coalesce fastest on open
           steppe; Wood Elves slowly in dense forest; Stone Giants barely at
           all in rugged terrain, with leadership forming around physical
           strength from the first season.
Year 7:    Adomorn moves Abaculus to beneath the Tarantar Mountains. Creatures
           of Olum begin expanding outward from that location over the following
           centuries. Adomorn begins building Heaven's Oak chamber with Wood
           Elves, at the settlement that will become Caldalus — the chamber sits
           in what becomes the capital, not in a remote place. Chooses fewer
           than 10 Wood Elf families as Guardians of the Tree, the first members
           being the heads of those families. The Guardians therefore predate
           the Wood Elf kingdom itself, which is why they sit outside normal
           Wood Elf society structure.
20-80:     WESTERN ALIOS: consolidation phase. The Wood Elf ADULT population is
           flat until roughly year 45 — children are born throughout, but no new
           claimants to land or leadership arrive, so there is no scarcity and
           they organize by negotiation to reach High cohesion. Stone Giant numbers
           climb from year 9 onward; rival holdings entrench and Low cohesion
           locks in by about year 50. Dark Dwarves consolidate early and reach
           High cohesion.
30-60:     WESTERN ALIOS: the Wood Elf minimal-impact philosophy is FORMALIZED
           as the first permanent clearings become established features. The
           underlying affinity is inherited from the Drasu and was never
           absent; what these years produce is articulated practice — what may
           be taken, in what quantity, and what is owed back. No guilt is
           involved. Replanting at the forest edges follows from the same
           inheritance.
~40-60:    WESTERN ALIOS: first contact between Wood Elves and
           Stone Giants. The Giants come downhill under population pressure.
           The elves answer with magic. A permanent stalemate forms that
           neither side can break, and the Stone Giant raid cycle begins.
Year 41:   WESTERN ALIOS: the Dark Dwarves sink the first deep shaft in the
           northern Forbidden Peaks. The first of Hellar's two founding dates
           — the crown's date.
Year 54:   WESTERN ALIOS: the first great hall of Hellar is cut and the moot
           convenes underground for the first time. The name Hellar attaches
           from this point. The second of Hellar's two founding dates — the
           moot's date. Which of the two founded the city has been argued ever
           since, and the argument is a dispute over whether the assembly or
           the crown holds primacy.
~60-150:   WESTERN ALIOS: the Dark Dwarf timber frontier expands, weighted
           EAST and inland rather than north along the arc, because the eastern
           haul is lower, warmer and downhill. The northern slope settlements
           stay small and marginal. The boreal margins are stripped.
~100-150:  WESTERN ALIOS: first contact between Wood Elves and
           Dark Dwarves, who come seeking timber after stripping their own
           boreal woodland. They arrive with a proposition rather than a
           threat. Dark Dwarf tools reach Faelyn and the great building age of
           Caldalus follows. The eastern haul line is what brought them there.
~150-221:  WESTERN ALIOS: the timber-for-metal trade
           establishes. The Wood Elves refuse to sell charcoal, permanently
           capping Dark Dwarf smelting. The Dark Dwarves go progressively
           deeper underground and the adaptation of their eyes begins.
Year 221:  First Ice Dwarf expeditions depart Icefall in multiple directions.
           One expedition sails south and makes landfall at the northern end
           of the arc on the edges of Stone Giant territory. The encounters are
           tense and any peace is short-lived, and they decline to settle there.
           The fleet continues south and west along the outer coast, finds
           unoccupied ground, settles, and shortly afterward meets the Dark
           Dwarves. That settlement becomes KULDOR. Trade routes established
           here build friendly relations with the Dark Dwarves and in time the
           Wood Elves — not with the Giants.
           A SEPARATE AND INDEPENDENT expedition sails in a different direction
           and discovers the Tarantar Mountains and their mineral wealth,
           sending word back to Icefall. The two ventures never informed one
           another.
Year 239:  Ice Dwarves establish Nusul in the Tarantar Mountains. Regular
           supply runs and labor forces begin. Creatures of Olum have been
           expanding in the mountains for 232 years.
Year 253:  First major Olum encounter at Nusul. Dwarves fight them off and
           continue working. Valuable mineral deposits found as they dig
           deeper. Attacks grow more frequent as the population grows.
Year 261:  Major Olum defeat at Nusul. Part of the mines closed off.
           Reinforcements sent from Icefall. Permanent guards and scouts
           deployed. Expansion attempts consistently end in being pushed back.
Year 268:  A mine at Nusul punches through into a large subterranean cavern
           swarming with Creatures of Olum. The kingdom is overrun and all
           inhabitants killed. Later that same year the last supply ship
           arrives from Icefall, finds silence and Olum, and immediately
           returns to Icefall. The Ice Dwarves never return to Nusul.
1206-1214: Dragon Wars. Harrad formed under King Eisengol.
Year 1529: Bourbac takes throne. Iron Dwarf enslavement begins. Herringer
           resistance begins under Arrama Herringer, protected by Lord Yllyndel
           the Ancient (Plains Elves).
Year 1561: Bourbac assassinated. Evander "Freeman" takes throne. Frees Iron
           Dwarves. Arrama Herringer eventually killed by bandits.
Year 2059: Arias Graymist born (Felgard, Utoa).
Year 2111: Harbinger Set created. Cobalt Dragonborne raids on Felgard cease
           by 2114.
Year 2136: Greyhawk finds Abaculus in Tarantar Mountains (short-range Olum
           anomaly). Corrupted on contact. Through Greyhawk's memories the
           Abaculus learns of Heaven's Oak and the Guardians of the Tree.
           Greyhawk deliberately relocates Abaculus to beneath grasslands near
           Rohl — knowing Rohl will be the site of the war's most pivotal
           battle. The Abaculus will draw Olum to the city while Greyhawk
           abducts creatures for corruption and builds his forces. Begins
           building forces for the Great War — recruiting willing dragons and
           forcibly corrupting those who refuse at Olum nests. Creatures of
           Olum in the Tarantar Mountains begin to disperse without the
           Abaculus's pull to anchor them.
Year 2140: Arias Graymist dies (age 81). Sons inherit Harbinger Set.
2140-2148: Felgard-Dragonborne peace treaty brokered by Cyrus Graymist.
Year 2148: Great War — first attacks begin. Lord Yllyndel the Ancient dies
           sometime during this conflict.
Year 2165: Battle of Rohl. When the battle turns against him, Greyhawk flees
           attempting to reach the Abaculus to weaponize it against Karma and
           Adomorn — the same fatal miscalculation Ah'Tabai made. Karma and
           Adomorn pursue and kill him before he reaches it. Adomorn does not
           know that was Greyhawk's intention and therefore does not know the
           Abaculus is nearby. In the final instant, the Abaculus acts through
           the dying Greyhawk — his dying curse transforms Karma into a
           golden-skinned Kobold and binds him to the Burnt Plains directly
           above the Abaculus, as the intended vessel for Greyhawk's
           resurrection. Morgan, Victoria, Nathan killed. Harrad dissolves.
           Common Calendar resets to year 0.
Year 2190: Cyrus Graymist dies (age 86). Tomb near Jorvik. Staff believed
           still there.

--- 3RD EPOCH (AW = AFTER WAR) ---
116 AW:  Valindra and Cyran, young Plains Elves from a nomadic settlement near
         a small lake in Ellendale, drown when their boat fails far from shore.
         Neither were strong swimmers. Cyran does not remain. Valindra's spirit
         inhabits the lake. The nomadic settlement moves on shortly after. The
         lake eventually becomes known as Valindra's Torment.
244 AW:  Aer'Raenal Liaphyra born (Wood Elf).
333 AW:  Degeric Cent born (Hillfolk human).
347 AW:  Mya Li born (Caldalus, Faelyn). Aer'Raenal is 103 at her birth.
352 AW:  Diquyk Lockwood born (central Alios, Hillfolk).
354 AW:  Maeglin Leralonde born (High Elf, T'sarra).
355 AW:  Tarik Bolden born.
357 AW:  Phelloden Liaphyra born. Aer'Raenal is 113 at his birth.
366 AW:  Diquyk kills parents and town guards with blue flame (age 14). Taken
         in by Degeric Cent, paladin of Angellia, who knew the family and was
         among those who came running at the disturbance.
369 AW:  Galstaff Quint born (human).
372 AW:  Grimmon Herringer born (Iron Dwarf).
~376 AW: Diquyk parts from Degeric — a mutual recognition that his potential
         was larger than his small town. Degeric points him toward a region of
         rumored Olum activity — nest hunting — as direction for the next
         chapter of his life. TENTATIVE — specific region undeveloped. Begins
         creating Jaunt Gates.
379-381 AW: Diquyk visits Degeric one last time — still pre-corruption.
             Degeric dies 381 AW (age 48). Never knew what his student became.
383 AW:  Diquyk, drawn by curiosity to the Burnt Plains — a patch of dead earth
         receiving normal rainfall yet supporting no life, an unnatural anomaly
         in the fertile terrain of central Kimora — investigates. The Abaculus
         senses his power and guides him to the hidden entry point among the
         battlefield debris. Corrupted on contact. Leaves the Burnt Plains
         immediately after corruption.
383-387 AW: Diquyk recruits the two forces that will prosecute the Elf War.
         Visits Stone Giants of the Tempest Peaks first — demonstrates
         overwhelming magical power, offers plunder and the riches of Faelyn,
         promises to return with additional forces. Then travels to The Deep
         and recruits the Dark Elves, equipping them with magical and enchanted
         weapons. Places Jaunt Gates near both locations. Directs operations
         from between the two kingdoms throughout the early war years.
385 AW:  Balric Silverfox born (human).
386 AW:  Tarik Bolden takes the throne of Kimora at age 31. Immediately begins
         raiding villages and enslaving populations.
387 AW:  Gralvin Sutton born. Elf War begins. Dark Elf and Stone Giant forces
         begin a two-front assault on Faelyn — mixed units probing from the
         north through the Tempest Peaks and from the east out of The Deep.
         Mya Li joins the militia at age 40, prompted by word of Stone Giant
         attacks on the outskirts of Faelyn. Kharis Ailwin joins at essentially
         the same time — among the survivors of a Stone Giant attack on a Wood
         Elf village, his family killed in that attack.
389 AW:  Galstaff begins traveling at age 20. Discovers Nexus on Myst.
         Reads Diquyk's records. Learns to safely use the Jaunt Gates.
392 AW:  Diquyk makes his first major push on the Wood Elves. First large
         encounter — Mya's most crushing defeat. Kharis Ailwin killed alongside
         several others under her command. Mya reports back. Aer'Raenal urges
         Mya to seek out Adomorn. Adomorn, based on Mya's account, suspects
         Diquyk is a Magni — but does not anticipate how far his powers have
         developed. Mya returns to Faelyn with Adomorn. A council of war —
         Aer'Raenal, Adomorn, Mya, and Faelyn's tacticians and strategists —
         decides to abandon the defensive posture and go on the offensive. Mya
         volunteers to lead the attack and calls openly for volunteers rather
         than selecting a unit. Ten come forward, all understanding it is
         likely a suicide mission. Marauders formed (10 total). Mya leads 8
         Marauders into The Deep as a deliberate decoy force. Adomorn takes 2 Marauders
         through a Jaunt Gate deep within Dark Elf territory, putting them to
         sleep for transit, to flank from behind. Diquyk senses the gate
         transit and is tipped off. Diquyk intercepts Adomorn's spells and
         redirects them as blue flame, killing multiple Marauders. Mya defeats
         and kills Shedyak, acquires the Bow of the Dales. When Mya and Adomorn
         switch to physical combat, cutting off Diquyk's fuel supply, a barrier
         goes up around him — near-reflexive, the body's answer to a beating
         that had nearly killed him. Adomorn tears open a doorway into the
         banishment plane. Diquyk, too physically broken to react, fails to
         intercept it, and the two push him through still sealed inside his own
         shell. Adomorn closes it behind him. All 10 Marauders dead. Only Mya and Adomorn survive
         the battle itself. TENTATIVE: Adomorn, badly wounded, dies shortly
         after banishing Diquyk — eventually rematerializes at Atral. Mya,
         unaware of his true nature, believes him dead. Diquyk banished to a
         separate physical parallel world where time moves more slowly. He is
         40 years old at banishment.
394 AW:  Gralvin's village destroyed by Tarik (age 7). Taken as gladiatorial slave.
399 AW:  Bor Glitterbeard born (Iron Dwarf, Fardar).
408 AW:  Gralvin (21) and Balric (23) escape together. Directed to Valeria.
         Gralvin and Valeria become friends, then lovers. Gralvin and Balric
         choose to stay and help her network.
~408-412 AW: Grimmon Herringer travels to Ellendale then Faelyn. Plains Elf
             emissary accompanies Grimmon to Faelyn. Grimmon tells Aer'Raenal
             and Mya firsthand stories of Tarik's atrocities. Mya volunteers
             to help. Aer'Raenal dispatches her.
413 AW:  Mya arrives at Valeria's — pointed there by the same network.
         Everything crystallizes. Simultaneous strikes by Plains Elves and
         Herringers across Kimora. Tarik overthrown and killed. Valeria dies
         in the final battle. Gralvin takes the throne at age 26. Iron Dwarves
         freed and return to Fardar. Grimmon Herringer (age 41) present.
427 AW:  Diquyk returns to Lanamyr through the Nexus on Myst, having spent
         approximately 1 subjective year in the banishment plane while 35
         years passed in Lanamyr. Physically aged to approximately 41 despite
         being 75 by Lanamyr reckoning. Finds his former work on Myst abandoned
         or gone. Brief flicker of hurt and fear before Abaculus redirects it.
         Returns to Burnt Plains. Begins working toward Greyhawk's resurrection.
429 AW:  Grimmon Herringer killed by Orc raiders (instruments of the Kimora
         framing operation). Age 57. Bor obtains the map from Grimmon's
         belongings.
430 AW:  Adenburry sinkhole event. Subterranean Creatures of Olum breach the
         town. Wilward's airship catches fire and tears in half. Bor and
         Qenwick, traveling overland from Fardar, are also present. The group
         fights together and exterminate the nest. Alexa eventually makes her
         way to Broldendul. Wilward stays in Adenburry to help rebuild. Maeglin,
         Bor, Skitter, and Qenwick travel together to inform Herringer members
         across Kimora of Grimmon's death. Qenwick returns to Fardar after.
432 AW:  PRESENT DAY. Diquyk (80 by Lanamyr reckoning, physically ~46) pursues
         Greyhawk's resurrection via Karma at the Burnt Plains. Mya Li (age 85)
         adventures with Balric Silverfox. Maeglin (age 78), Bor (age 33), and
         Skitter travel together pursuing the lost kingdom. Adomorn at Tower of
         Grannel. Gralvin (age 45) rules Kimora. Galstaff Quint (age 63)
         travels. Aer'Raenal (age 188) rules Faelyn. Estefania (age 23) lives
         somewhere on Alios. Qenwick in Fardar. Wilward in Adenburry rebuilding.
         Alexa leads Four Crowns in Broldendul. Elsie (age 53) and Alita (age
         24) Varis run the Monowi Inn. A Relic of Creation sits in a glass vial
         around a Kobold's neck. Valindra walks the shore of her lake.
