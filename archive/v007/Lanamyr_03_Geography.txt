GEOGRAPHY AND WORLD STRUCTURE
================================================================================

CONTINENTS:
Lanamyr has three major continents. Alios and Edura are joined by land and
together form one large landmass. Utoa is the only major continent not connected
to the others — it is entirely surrounded by ocean. All other landmasses are
islands of varying size.

ALIOS:
The largest continent. Dominates the northern and central portion of the world.
Has a large northern peninsula (Andar), a western arm containing the Forbidden
Peaks and Tempest Peaks, and an eastern extension toward the Westwind Mountains.
Central Alios is predominantly Deciduous and Mixed Forest, transitioning to
Steppes and Cool Desert in the east. The western arm receives high rainfall and
contains dense forests. The northern regions are cold, with the Tarantar
Mountains being the highest and coldest range in Lanamyr.

EDURA:
Considerably smaller than Alios. Connected to Alios via the Blackwing Hills
land corridor in the southwest — geographically similar to how Europe and Asia
are separate continents but joined by land. Northern Edura is tropical rain
forest, home to T'sarra and the ruins of Atral. Central Edura contains the
Void Sands desert. Southern Edura cools progressively toward tundra at its
southern tip.

UTOA:
The only isolated major continent, surrounded entirely by ocean. Has a prominent
east-west mountain range — the Desolate Tops — of very high elevation. Northwestern
Utoa is Steppes and Cool Desert terrain, home to Felgard. Eastern Utoa contains
the Evergreen Strand. Southern Utoa reaches Polar Ice and Polar Desert at its
extreme southern end.

MAJOR GEOGRAPHIC FEATURES — ALIOS:
- Tarantar Mountains: Northern Alios, running east-west. The tallest mountain
  range in Lanamyr — peaks reach extreme elevation (gray on elevation map).
  Cold Parklands and Boreal biomes. Home of The Deep (Dark Elf underground
  kingdom) and Cave Gnomes beneath the mountains.
- THE WESTERN ARC (confirmed; verified against Lanamyr_Features.jpg and
  Lanamyr_Biomes.jpg in session v006): the Forbidden Peaks, the Darkened Slopes
  and the Tempest Peaks are not three separate features. They form ONE
  CONTINUOUS HIGHLAND ARC running along the outer west and north coast of the
  western arm, and the Wood Elf forest sits in the bowl enclosed by it. The arc
  runs dry at its southwest end and grades wetter and colder to the northeast —
  thorn scrub, then steppe, then boreal moist forest. Dark Dwarves hold the
  southwest end, Stone Giants the north end, Wood Elves the interior bowl.
  Along much of the western coast the range comes close to the sea, leaving
  only a narrow coastal shelf between the cliffs and the water. See
  Kingdoms_History for the consequences of this geometry, which include the
  fact that the Dark Dwarves and Stone Giants never met during the founding era.
- The Tempest Peaks: Northern end of the western arc, along the north coast of
  the western arm. Significant elevation. Origin of Stone Giants. High rainfall,
  tundra at peaks.
- The Darkened Slopes: The middle stretch of the western arc, running northeast
  up the west coast from the Forbidden Peaks toward the Tempest Peaks. Boreal
  moist forest along the northern slopes and inland to the east — the timber
  the Dark Dwarves stripped in the first century of the 2nd Epoch. Thinly
  settled. The great east-west road crosses this stretch to reach the coast at
  Kuldor, and that crossing is the most dangerous leg of the route.
- The Forbidden Peaks: Southwest end of the western arc. Home of Hellar,
  the Dark Dwarf underground kingdom.
- The Black Mountains of Fardar: Central-eastern Alios, running east-west.
  Divided into western/central bulk (Iron Dwarf territory, Kingdom of Fardar)
  and eastern end (Tunneler Kobold origin territory). A river valley divides
  the eastern and western halves — site of the ancient Draak settlement.
- The Wandering Hills: Northeastern Alios, forming part of Kimora's border
  with Fardar. Origin of Screecher Goblins.
- Ash Mountains: Central Alios, running east-west. Form the southern border
  of Kimora.
- Monowi Hills: Small hills in central Alios, part of the natural border line
  between Kimora and Ellendale. Location of the Monowi Inn.
- The Westwind Mountains: Far eastern peninsula of Alios. Near the Gold
  Dragonborne and Orc origin territories.
- Blackwing Hills: The geological land connection between Alios and Edura.
  Elevated but not dramatically so — hills rather than mountains, though
  significant enough to form a clear continental boundary. Located between
  the Void Sands of Edura to the east and the Obsidian Dragonborne swamp
  region of southwestern Alios to the west.

MAJOR GEOGRAPHIC FEATURES — EDURA:
- Troll's Breath Mountains: Northern Edura, south of the ruins of Atral and
  the Waywin River. Named after the Mountain Trolls who originated there.
  Significant elevation — reads cooler than surrounding tropical terrain due
  to altitude. Home of Mountain Troll underground caverns that connect into
  the outer edges of the ruins of Atral.
- The Void Sands: Central Edura. Hot desert, near-zero precipitation. Origin
  of Sand Dwarves.
- The Iron Peaks: Southern Edura. Origin of Woodland Gnomes nearby.
- Blackwing Hills: See Alios entry above — straddles the Alios-Edura connection.
- Waywin River: Major river running through northern Edura through the site of
  the ruins of Atral. The city of Atral was built along this river.

MAJOR GEOGRAPHIC FEATURES — UTOA:
- The Desolate Tops: Central Utoa. Very high elevation, among the tallest
  terrain in Lanamyr. Tundra biome.
- The Barren Run: Utoa. Nature and description undeveloped.
- The Evergreen Strand: Far eastern tip of Utoa. Mixed to Coniferous Forest
  biome — the name reflects the green forested character of the strand amid
  Utoa's otherwise harsher terrain. Origin of Hardback Kobolds.
- Coldwind Mountains: Southern Utoa. Significant elevation.

MAJOR GEOGRAPHIC FEATURES — ISLANDS:
- Gleaming Spires: Remote far western island. Polar Ice biome. Snow-capped
  peaks that catch sunlight from a distance and appear almost self-luminous —
  as though generating their own light. The name derives from this effect.
- Firethorn Peaks: Remote far western island. Contains multiple active
  volcanoes. The name reflects the fiery volcanic character of the peaks.
  Warmer biome tones due to volcanic activity despite southern latitude.

RIVERS:
Only major rivers are mapped at world scale. Countless unnamed rivers, streams,
and lakes exist throughout Lanamyr — these will be developed as needed.
- Waywin River: Northern Edura. Runs through the site of the ruins of Atral.

CONTINENTAL BIOME SUMMARY:
- Icefall: Polar Ice — extreme cold essential for Ice Forging
- Northern Alios: Cold Parklands and Boreal — harsh and cold
- Central Alios: Deciduous and Mixed Forests — fertile, temperate
- Eastern Alios: Steppes to Cool Desert — drier, more arid
- Western arm of Alios: Dense forest — high rainfall
- Southern Alios: Warming toward Tropical Seasonal Forest
- Northern Edura (T'sarra): Tropical Rain Forest — confirmed lush
- Central Edura (Void Sands): Hot Desert — arid
- Southern Edura tip: Tundra — cold and harsh
- Utoa (northwest): Steppes and Cool Desert
- Utoa (south): Polar Ice and Polar Desert
- Snow Elf island: Polar Ice and Polar Desert — extreme cold
- Malulo Archipelago: Tropical Rain Forest — warm and lush
- Gleaming Spires island: Polar Ice
- Firethorn Peaks island: Warmer due to active volcanism

KEY LOCATIONS
================================================================================

THE BURNT PLAINS: Vast swath of scorched, barren earth near ruins of Rohl,
within Kimora. Beneath lies the Abaculus. At the very center sits Karma — now
a golden-skinned Kobold — bound there by Greyhawk's dying curse. Lifeless after
400+ years — the result of Greyhawk's immense corruption leaking into the ground
as his body decayed after the Battle of Rohl. The landscape bears the physical
scars of that battle: craters, outcroppings, and rubble from the clash of
enormously powerful beings. Rainfall in the Burnt Plains is normal — the same
as the surrounding fertile Deciduous Forest terrain of central Kimora. Rain
falls and nothing grows. The lifelessness is not a product of drought or climate
but of deep corruption that defies natural recovery. This makes it a visible
anomaly on any biome or vegetation map — a dead patch surrounded by living land
that receives the same rainfall. It was precisely this unnatural quality that
drew Diquyk's curiosity in 383 AW. Adomorn, having been present at the battle,
understands Greyhawk's corruption as the cause and has no reason to investigate
further — it reads to him as a known scar. Hidden amongst the battlefield debris
is a narrow burrow or crawl space leading underground — small enough that only
a slight person could enter, up to 30 meters long, concealed among rubble and
outcroppings. The Abaculus subtly influences anyone who comes close to this
entry point to turn away. It guided Diquyk through it and into the purpose-built
chamber beneath, constructed by Greyhawk during the Great War.

CALDALUS: Capital of Faelyn, in the forested lowlands of the western arm of
Alios. The first and largest Wood Elf city. Built in both canopy and ground
construction, and in the present day a great deal of it sits high among the
trees — intricate, very well-built structures linked by networks of walkways
and bridges between the larger trees left standing.
THE TREE CHOSE THE SITE (confirmed, v007): Heaven's Oak was already standing,
and the gathering settlement Adomorn walked into in year 7 of the 2nd Epoch had
already formed around it. He chose the tree, not the settlement; the Wood Elves
there were simply the ones at hand, and the Guardian families were drawn from
them. Caldalus grew up around the Oak, and therefore around the chamber built
inside it. The Guardian lines are founding families of the capital as a
consequence of having lived near a particular tree.
WHY A GATHERING WAS ALREADY THERE: the Wood Elf scatter phase is a problem of
finding one another in terrain that limits sight and travel (see
Kingdoms_History). An enormous tree is the one thing in a forest that everyone
can find. The same property that made the Oak valuable to Adomorn — its sheer
living mass — made it the natural centre of a gathering, and ultimately of a
kingdom.
THE IRONY: Adomorn could not have had the one without the other. He selected
the best available cover in the region and in doing so guaranteed that a
capital city would eventually grow on top of the most dangerous chamber in
Lanamyr. Whether he foresaw this is UNDEVELOPED.
See Kingdoms_History, Year 7.
The mixed character was never a decision. Gathering and hauling the material
needed to build in the canopy produced large clearings as a byproduct, and
those clearings were then built into. The city became half canopy and half
ground one hauling road and one staging yard at a time, across centuries, while
the people building it believed they were being careful.
ORDER OF CONSTRUCTION (confirmed): the canopy came FIRST. The earliest Wood
Elf structures were built in the treetops, and ground construction followed
only after expansion had cleared the ground to build on. Caldalus did not begin
at ground level and grow upward; it began above and came down.
This gives Caldalus a visible geology, and it runs opposite to what a visitor
would assume. Its OLDEST work is its canopy. The open ground districts are the
LATER layer, opened as expanding canopy settlements demanded ever greater
quantities of timber and then built into once the ground stood clear.
No guilt attaches to this. The Wood Elves understood from the outset that
living in the forest meant taking from it, and they treated the clearings as a
cost to be accounted for rather than a wrong to be regretted. What the growth
of Caldalus produced was not shame but practice — the formalization, around
years 30 to 60 of the 2nd Epoch, of what may be taken and what is owed back.
The Wood Elves live long enough to have watched the city change, and they
regard its open districts plainly: this is what it took to build here.
See Races for the inherited caretaker disposition and for the replanting
carried out by smaller settlements at the forest edges.
The city's great building age followed the arrival of Dark Dwarf tools (roughly
years 100 to 150 of the 2nd Epoch). Good metal edges transformed felling,
shaping, and joinery. The restraint ethic was already established by then,
which is the only reason the forest survived the expansion.
See Races for Wood Elf canopy architecture and the minimal-impact philosophy.

HEAVEN'S OAK: Central feature of Caldalus, capital of Faelyn, containing a
chamber built under Adomorn's instruction in year 7 of the 2nd Epoch. Three
purposes: physical containment of Abaculus, masking its presence from Creatures
of Olum, and preventing it from acting as a universal beacon detectable by all
Irridae. Intact. Never held the Abaculus. Protected by the Guardians of the
Tree. Its true purpose is not widely known outside the Guardians in the present
day.

THE TREE ITSELF (confirmed, v007): Heaven's Oak is an ancient oak of
extraordinary size — approximately 70 feet in trunk diameter and approximately
300 feet tall. It is a natural tree. Nothing about it is supernatural, magical
or created; it is simply very old and very large.
WHY ADOMORN CHOSE IT: ambient energy is generated by all living things and
collectively masks the Abaculus (see Overview_Cosmology). A life form of this
mass emits a great deal of that noise. Adomorn selected the Oak for the cover
its sheer living bulk provides — a loud place to hide a quiet thing.
IT IS NOT UNIQUE, ONLY GREATEST. The central forests of present-day Faelyn are
full of very large trees. Heaven's Oak stands above them, but its neighbours
are themselves massive. AUTHOR'S CALIBRATION NOTE, not in-world prose: the
relationship is Everest to the Himalayas rather than a single giant on a plain.
This matters for concealment — the Oak is the largest of a class, not an
inexplicable anomaly demanding explanation.

THE CHAMBER (confirmed, v007 — SUPERSEDES the earlier "sunk into the earth at
the foot of the tree" description, which was a recording error):
The chamber is INSIDE THE TRUNK. It is cylindrical, set in the centre of the
trunk, approximately 30 feet wide and 30 feet tall, beginning a short distance
below ground level and extending up into the tree. The Oak is alive and
undamaged; the chamber occupies the heart of it.
THE ENTRANCE IS HIDDEN. It does not open at the base of the trunk. It runs
underground from elsewhere and comes up beneath the tree among the roots.
Nothing at ground level marks it.
THE INTERIOR WAS FASHIONED BY ADOMORN HIMSELF as a second shielding layer,
isolating the Abaculus from detection independently of the tree's own masking.
AUTHOR'S CALIBRATION NOTE, not in-world prose: the intended function is a
Faraday cage. The tree supplies ambient noise; the chamber interior supplies
isolation. Two different mechanisms stacked.
CONSEQUENCE FOR CONCEALMENT: from outside, Heaven's Oak looks like exactly what
a Wood Elf landmark should look like — an enormous tree in a forest of enormous
trees. There is no structure to notice. See Kingdoms_History, Year 7, for why
this makes the chamber a technological island of a different kind than
previously recorded.

WHAT THE WOOD ELVES BELIEVE (confirmed): as Caldalus grew, the proportion of
its people who knew anything true about the Oak shrank steadily, and the
Guardians kept the secret. After several generations the Oak was simply
accepted — no longer a thing requiring explanation. In the absence of one, the
Wood Elves supplied their own. Many hold some variation of the belief that
Heaven's Oak is the seed of the Wood Elves' creation, and that it may hold the
key to unlocking the memories lost from before. None of this is true. The
variations are many and no single orthodoxy exists.

THE IRONY: the belief is wrong in every particular and correct in instinct. The
Oak was built by the last living Drasu — the progenitor of the Wood Elves — to
contain the object whose arrival at Atral shattered the Drasu and made them.
It genuinely is bound up with their origin and with the thing that created
them. They have the right tree and the wrong story, and they will not learn
otherwise from the Guardians.

THE COVER WAS NEVER INVENTED: the Guardians did not fabricate a story to
conceal the chamber. Their people built one themselves, sincerely, and a
sincere belief holds far better than any fabrication. The Guardians' entire
concealment strategy amounts to declining to correct anyone.

ATTEMPTS ON THE MEMORIES (confirmed): the methods tried at Heaven's Oak over
the centuries are among the "methods tried" referenced in the Sundering memory
passage — see Overview_Cosmology. None reach beyond the boundary. The Guardians
know with certainty that none ever will, and stand watching their own people
try anyway.

TIMING NOTE: this mythology takes several Wood Elf generations to settle, and a
generation runs 75 to 80 years. It belongs to roughly year 250 of the 2nd Epoch
onward. Through the founding era of years 0 to 221 the Oak is recent history
rather than legend — see Kingdoms_History, Year 7. The Abaculus learned of this
chamber through Greyhawk's memories at the moment of his corruption in year
2136, 2nd Epoch. Knowledge of the chamber's existence and purpose is what
directed the Abaculus to use Diquyk to wage war on Faelyn — breaking the
Guardians and compromising the chamber was a strategic priority before Diquyk
pursued the larger mission.

THE NEXUS: Deep cavern in a remote part of Myst. Central hub for all Jaunt
Gates. Diquyk's records remain here. Discovered by Galstaff Quint.

ATRAL (RUINS): First and greatest Drasian city, built along Waywin River on
Edura. A vast sprawling city with both above ground and underground sections.
In the Sundering, the entire city was destroyed — above ground structures
flattened, underground sections buried and largely lost to time. Home of the
Drasian Vault (deep underground, at the center of the underground city).
The High Elves of T'sarra discovered the ruins some years after the Sundering
and began excavating. The site was contested by Mountain Trolls from the Troll's
Breath Mountains to the south. Sand Dwarves eventually allied with the High
Elves and pushed the Trolls back. Centuries of excavation by the Var Harma have
uncovered significant portions but much remains buried. Rumored present-day
headquarters of the Var Harma.

ROHL (RUINS): Former capital and largest city of Harrad. Within present-day
Kimora. Palace destroyed during the Great War. Locals avoid it.

HERRINGTON: Mining town in Ash Mountains founded by Grimmon Herringer.
Emerald mines. Future uncertain after Grimmon's death.

BROLDENDUL: Town north of Herrington. Site of Orc raiding party attack (Orcs
dressed as Kimoran soldiers). Home of the Four Crowns gang.

ADENBURRY: Border town of Kimora near the Fardar border. In 430 AW a large
sinkhole opened in the center of town when subterranean Creatures of Olum
breached the surface. A vast excavation was discovered beneath. The nest was
exterminated by a group of adventurers who were present at the time. Several
buildings caught fire during the collapse. Wilward Cobbersplint's airship,
which had landed in Adenburry, caught fire and tore in half as the ground gave
way. Wilward remained in Adenburry to help the town rebuild and to reconstruct
his airship. The Adenburry sinkhole event was not connected to the Abaculus —
it was an independent natural Olum nesting occurrence.

TOWER OF GRANNEL: Adomorn's base of operations in the present day.

DRASIAN VAULT: Underground vault built by the Drasu at the center of the
underground city of Atral on Edura. Contains records of the Irridae and the
nature of Relics of Creation, along with the most valuable and most dangerous
of the Drasu's holdings. Survived the Sundering intact. Located deep beneath
centuries of debris and collapse — not easily reached.
SEALED (CONFIRMED): The Vault has never been opened since the Sundering. It
will open only in the presence of a Drasu. No amount of excavation, force, or
ingenuity will breach it. Adomorn is the last surviving Drasu and therefore
the only living being who can open it.
Whether the Var Harma have located the Vault by 432 AW is undecided, but the
question matters less than it once did — locating it and opening it are not
the same problem. Adomorn is aware of their excavations at Atral and is
untroubled by them, precisely because he knows they cannot get in. The dangerous
knowledge is behind a door only he can open.

MONOWI HILLS: A small section of hills in the natural border line between
Kimora and Ellendale. The broader border between the two kingdoms is defined
by an unnamed line of hills and small mountains running from the Ash Mountains
in the south up toward the Tarantar Mountains in the north. This line has no
single collective name. The Monowi Hills are one portion of that line — the
area where Elsie Varis's farm and inn are located.

MONOWI INN: A small inn built next to Elsie Varis's farmhouse in the Monowi
Hills near the Kimora-Ellendale border. Officially called the Monowi Inn;
frequent travelers simply call it "Elsie's place." One of the most well known
and frequently visited stops along the road between Fardar and Faelyn. Run by
Elsie Varis with her daughter Alita. See character entries for details.

KULDOR: Port city on the west coast of the western arm of Alios, at the foot
of the Darkened Slopes. One of the largest port cities and trading hubs in
Alios, and the western terminus of the great east-west road.

THE SETTING AND LAYOUT (confirmed): the coastal range runs close to the sea
along this stretch, leaving a shelf of open ground roughly half a mile deep
between the waterline and the cliffs. Kuldor occupies all of it. The port
district fills that shelf — docks and warehouses at the water, then shops,
inns and housing stretching back across the flat until the ground ends at a
sheer vertical wall of rock. Set into that cliff face is a large and
intricately carved stone entrance, worked to be seen from the whole length of
the shelf. Through it the city continues, running back into the mountain and
down below sea level. Everything past the threshold is Dark Dwarf work. It is
nowhere near the scale of Hellar, but it is accomplished on its own terms, and
for most travellers in Lanamyr it is the only Dark Dwarf architecture they will
ever stand inside.
THE ENTRANCE IS ADVERTISING. A people whose outsider disposition is
transactional, and who are the only western Alios race that travels TO others,
put a monumental carved threshold facing a working port. It is not decoration.
It is the trade pitch, and it has been standing there doing its job for
centuries.
CUTTING BELOW SEA LEVEL beside an ocean is a feat, and exactly the sort of
problem a people who perfect rather than invent across centuries would be good
at and proud of. Hellar is deeper, but Hellar never had to keep the sea out.

HISTORY: founded by the Ice Dwarf expedition of year 221, 2nd Epoch, after they
declined to settle on the edges of Stone Giant territory further north and
continued south and west along the coast to unoccupied ground. Some of the small
Dark Dwarf northern timber settlements lay in the same region, and as both
populations grew the two peoples developed a relationship, most likely through
trade. Something later forced the Dark Dwarf settlements to merge into Kuldor
for protection — CAUSE AND TIMING BOTH UNDEVELOPED; the Dragon Wars (1206–1214,
2nd Epoch) are a strong candidate, and Stone Giants are another. Creatures of
Olum have been ruled out for this event.
WHAT THE MERGE COST HELLAR: Kuldor absorbed those settlements rather than the
reverse. Hellar lost its entire northern presence in a single stroke and gained
a permanent expatriate population living under someone else's roof. The Dark
Dwarves of Kuldor live beside the sea and trade with five races daily; the Dark
Dwarves of Hellar live far to the south at the bottom of a shaft. Same race,
same language, two different lives.

WHY IT GREW: Kuldor is a CHOKEPOINT, not a workshop. Ice Forged tools and
weapons cannot be made here — the technique is locked to Icefall by its ore and
its cold (see Races). Kuldor is the single mainland point where they reliably
come ashore, and the only one Icefall controls. Sky Gnome inventions are the
other pillar; Myst lies almost due west of the western arm at nearly the same
latitude, making Kuldor the nearest mainland port to the Sky Gnomes and their
natural gateway to the continent.
AFTER NUSUL: Icefall lost its northern venture entirely in 268 and kept this
one. From that point Kuldor is Icefall's only foreign holding.

PRESENT DAY (432 AW): mostly Ice and Dark Dwarves, with sizeable populations of
Sky Gnomes, humans and Wood Elves. Water Whisperer shipwrights are sought after
in any port city and Kuldor is no exception — Jerrol Beggar lives here. See
Characters & Items.
GOVERNANCE: UNDEVELOPED. Kuldor would be the first polity in Lanamyr that is
not a race-kingdom.

THE ROAD: A major trade route running roughly east to west across central
Alios and out to the western ocean. Departs Fardar in the east, crosses Kimora,
then Ellendale, then Faelyn, then climbs over the Darkened Slopes to end at
Kuldor on the west coast. Bisects Kimora roughly in half. One of the heaviest
trade routes in the region, servicing many stops along the way. Adenburry and
Broldendul sit near the road on the eastern side of Kimora close to Fardar.
The Monowi Inn sits near the road on the western side of Kimora close to the
Ellendale border. Caldalus is the last major stop before the mountains.
THE NAME (resolved in v006): the road goes by multiple names. It is long
enough that no one calls the whole of it anything, and the people who use a
stretch name the stretch. The two most common names are WANDERER'S ROAD and
THE MERCHANT'S SPINE.
THE FINAL CROSSING: the last leg, over the Darkened Slopes, is dangerous. It is
the one piece of the western arc anybody is obliged to cross, through country
that was never properly settled and is now largely empty, its population having
consolidated into Kuldor. Large caravans generally travel with a contingent of
guards or mercenaries, and wealthy tradesmen hire more of them and better ones.
This supports a standing trade in armed escort at both ends of the crossing.
What preys there: UNDEVELOPED. Whether the crossing carries a name of its own —
likely given by the people who survived it rather than the people who mapped it
— is UNDEVELOPED.

VALINDRA'S TORMENT: A small lake somewhere in the plains of Ellendale. Unnamed
before the tragedy of 116 AW. The nomadic settlement that once sat near its
shore — mostly Plains Elves and humans — moved on shortly after the drownings.
The lake has carried the name Valindra's Torment ever since, given by locals
who felt what lingered there. Plains Elves and others who have settled in that
region over the centuries are careful to avoid it. Those who wander close feel
Valindra's grief and torment as though it were their own. Reports of a vengeful
or evil spirit circulate among travelers — the details exaggerated by retelling.
The truth is simpler and sadder: she is still waiting for Cyran.
