## Class Features — Level 1

### Favored Enemy: Dragons
Advantage on Survival checks to track dragons and on Intelligence checks to
recall information about them. Learns **Draconic**.

**In-fiction note:** Sharii cannot explain this affinity. It is not ancestral —
Wood Elves are Drasu-descended and dragons are Draak-descended, so no
hereditary link exists. Whatever the source, it is stranger than heredity.
See Character section below.

*Draconic is used as a table placeholder. The bible confirms dragons have their
own language but never names it — logged in `reference/lore-deltas.md`.*

### Natural Explorer: Forest
In forest terrain: difficult terrain doesn't slow the group's travel; cannot
become lost except by magic; remains alert to danger even while tracking,
foraging, or navigating; can move stealthily at a normal pace when alone; finds
twice as much food when foraging; when tracking, learns the creatures' exact
number, sizes, and how long ago they passed.

### Working vocabulary (established session 2)

Eight days in Caldalus, three *Speak with Animals* castings a day, spent finding
out what Knight already understands rather than teaching him obedience. The
discovery that reframed it: Knight had already assigned himself a job — *be
between Sharii and the thing* — and every attempt to train him out of it failed
while everything built inside it worked immediately.

**No change to the statblock.** What exists now is fiction that functions
without the spell:

- **Flat hand, palm down** — stop and go quiet. Instant, held, reliable.
- **Two fingers and a direction** — go there and wait. Reliable inside ~40 ft;
  unreliable beyond it, and he will abandon it if he thinks Sharii has moved.
- **Low whistle through the teeth** — come back now, no questions.
- **Knight's own signal:** a hard, silent shoulder-press against Sharii's leg,
  meaning *something is wrong and I am not going to explain.* He did it twice on
  the meadow shelf. Sharii treats it as an order.

**Known limits:** cannot count past three; cannot tell dangerous from merely
present except by smell and movement; will not go where he cannot smell Sharii;
is four months old and chases cats.

**And one thing nobody asked for:** on the seventh day, Unbidden brushed Sharii
during a drill and Knight's head came up and looked straight at *him*, not at a
sound. **The wolf can tell when it happens.**

## House Rule — Unbidden

Sharii has an innate, **involuntary** sensitivity to magic. It is not a spell:
no slot, no action, no components. He cannot invoke it, suppress it, or control
it. **The DM decides when it fires; the player never rolls for it or requests
it.**

It gives sensation rather than information — direction, rough intensity, a
flavor of the thing. Rarely the school, never the name. Strong or unfamiliar
magic may leave him shaken or briefly unable to speak.

It fires at inconvenient moments, it is unreliable as intelligence, and — because
it is untrained and uncontrolled — it is **louder** in the ambient current than
deliberate casting would be. See `../house-rules.md` for full text and design
intent.

## Character

**The twin dynamic.** Sharii is the elder by a few minutes and has spent his
life following Alinar rather than leading him. He looks up to his younger
brother. Enlisting as a Ranger alongside him in 387 AW is continuous with that
pattern, not a break from it.

**The pull.** Sharii has always felt drawn toward magic — toward the ambient
energy, toward what a sorcerer does. He has never embraced it. He resisted that
pull deliberately in order to follow Alinar's path instead, and he still
struggles against it.

**DM NOTE — OPEN AND DELIBERATELY UNRESOLVED.**
Whether Sharii is a Magni is **not decided** and is not to be decided by
declaration. It surfaces through play: moments where he reaches for something
without meaning to, where the ambient current sits closer to him than it should
for someone with no training. The players interpret these as they like. Do not
confirm or deny.

Relevant pressures, for DM awareness only:
- The true nature of Magni is not generally understood anywhere in Lanamyr.
  Sharii would have no framework for what is happening to him, and neither
  would anyone around him.
- Diquyk's signature ability is **sensing others drawing on ambient energy** —
  it is how he detected Adomorn's Jaunt Gate transit in the final battle. A
  militia recruit who keeps almost-touching that current is not necessarily
  invisible to the man directing the war.
- `Lanamyr_08_Open_Questions.txt` reserves an unfilled slot for a more recent
  Magni, born within Aer'Raenal's lifetime and less powerful than Diquyk, to
  give the 392 AW council of war a shared reference point. That slot is open.
- If pursued: Wood Elves are Drasu-descended, so Sharii would be a Drasu-line
  Magni — the higher-ceiling kind. This does **not** explain the dragon
  affinity, which must have some other source.

**Appearance.** Average height for a Wood Elf male (roughly 5'7"), lean, light
tan skin, dark brown hair, dark eyes. Nothing about him stands out. You could
not pick him out of a crowd — and he and his brother are difficult to tell
apart at a glance.

**Alinar knows.** His brother is aware of the thing that happens to Sharii and
has been for their whole lives. He does not understand it. He has no more
framework for it than Sharii does. It is simply a fact about his brother that
the two of them have never had adequate words for.

**Why he enlisted, 387 AW.** The twins grew up in Caldalus. As the Stone Giant
attacks in the north began, they watched messengers and refugees come into the
city asking for help. Alinar decided to lend a hand. Sharii went with him,
because that is what Sharii does.

He did not enlist out of conviction. He enlisted because his brother did. Over
five years of war, whether that becomes conviction of his own is an open
question and a live thread.

**Personality, ideals, bonds, flaws:** undeveloped as declared traits — and
deliberately so. See **`behavior-log.md`** for the dated record of what he has
actually done, which is what the DM should reason from.
