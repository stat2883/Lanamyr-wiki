# Oris Caul

**Campaign-invented.** Not bible canon. First appeared **session 9**, 23 January
387 AW, Caldalus. See `../reference/lore-deltas.md`.

---

## Identity

- Wood Elf, male. **Looks middle years; is older.** Has held his warrant
  **fifty-nine years** and was already doing the work before it was formally his.
- Plain dark coat, good cloth, worn indoors in a house with no fire. **No seal
  anywhere on him. No servant. A flat case, never a satchel.** **Ink on the heel
  of the left hand** — a left-handed man who writes a great deal.
- Lives alone in a **narrow three-storey house in an unfashionable quarter of
  Caldalus**, between a cooper's and a boarded frontage, a tannery three doors
  along. Unlocks it himself with a key on a cord round his neck.
- **Ground floor:** a table, two chairs, a cot, a lamp, a cold grate — and
  paper, boxes and shelving floor to ceiling, with more stacked up the stairs.
  **The upper two floors are shuttered and the shutters have weathered shut.**
- **No cooking. No second person's scent, ever.** *(Observed by Ignis as a dog.)*

---

## The Warrant

A **royal household writ**, one of perhaps six or eight in existence. **Not
entered in the schedule of warrants** — the front matter of the plan-office
standing orders says *warrants of the royal household are not entered in this
register*, which is why it cannot be looked up and why its seal carries only
**two word-breaks** instead of an office's four, five or six.

**Entered in the household book under one word: *correspondence*.**

- **Renewed nine times by Aer'Raenal**, in his own hand, as an annual formality.
- **Older than those nine renewals.** The king was handed it by his father's
  steward in his first year, with the things a new king is told once and never
  given on paper.
- **The collection is 231 years old. Three kings. Three holders. Caul is the
  third**, and the oldest item in his box came to him with the box, entered in a
  hand that died before he was born.

### His instructions, kept fifty-nine years
> **You collect. You enter. You tell no one.**

### ⚠ And the fourth thing
Not an instruction. Said once, in a corridor, by the steward who handed him the
box, and **never repeated to a living soul until 23 January 387 AW**:

> ### ***"Don't try to read it."***

---

## What He Collects — the rule

**He does not want *cut*. He wants *unidentifiable*.** Maevis derived this in ten
minutes off his rejects: every cut item he has put back is in a script somebody
could name (foundry mark, tally, potter's token, a shopping list in plain
Aiwiya). Cut is the side effect — an unknown hand on soft material is exactly
what loses its direction and ends up in dead stock.

**He has never once taken a thing written with a pen.** *(Serry Vane,
thirty-one years.)*

**Where he looks:**
- **The college annex**, two or three times a year, **for at least eleven
  years** — longer than the current porter has had the door.
- **The dead counter** at the grain office, two or three days after quarter-day,
  missed perhaps four times in thirty-one years. Looks for twenty minutes.
  Usually takes nothing.
- **More often in the last two or three years**, between quarters. His question:
  ***"has anything unlettered come in."***

**He signs nothing. He has never been asked a single question about any of it by
anybody, including the three men who signed the warrant.**

---

## ⚠ The Box — nineteen items

Nineteen pieces of **the mountain hand**, out of perhaps four hundred
unidentifiable things on his shelves. Lead, wax, pale stone, fired clay, birch
bark, bone. **All cut, pressed or scratched. None inked.**

**⚠ FOUR OF THE NINETEEN ARE IDENTICAL TO ONE ANOTHER** — a lead strip, a wax
board, a clay tile and a thin slate, four materials across four separate
centuries of entries, **the same marks in the same order with the same breaks.**

**They are the nine-line form.** Ignis told him what they were; he had kept them
sixty years without knowing.

> ***"Then somebody was leaving them blank boards. In Kuldor. And off the
> southern river. And out of the east. For two hundred years. And I have entered
> every one of them in a ledger and sent the ledger up and never once been asked
> a question about it by anybody."***

**Known origins of items he has taken** *(from Serry Vane's road-and-date book,
covering only her thirty-one years)*: Kuldor caravan ×2, the river south ×2, a
southern carter's lot ×1, **and the east ×2** — the most recent nineteen months
old, a strip of birch bark out of a returned soldier's kit from Fardar way.

---

## ⚠ He Cannot Read It

Stated flat, before anybody's face could change, and twice over:

> *"Yes. I know this hand."* … *"**I cannot read it. Not one character.** Neither
> could the man who had this before me, and neither could his. I will not be the
> thing you hoped I was."*

Against which: **Teveren Ost watched his eyes track a line of it** — left to
right, back, and down — and said so with the disclaimer attached first, *"I may
be inventing it, and that is exactly how a man invents a memory."*

**Do not resolve this. Both halves stand.** Recognition and reading are not the
same act, and a man who has handled nineteen specimens for sixty years would
know the direction of a line without knowing a word of it.

---

## How To Play Him

**He is not a villain and he is not a mystery to be cracked.** He is a
scrupulous, isolated, very old clerk who has done one errand faithfully for
three kings, has never been told what it was for, and has just found out that it
meant something.

- **He is frightened of being in trouble, not of being found out.** *"At my age
  the waiting is the part that does the damage."*
- **He volunteers his own position before being accused of anything.**
- **He is courteous without being charming.** Serry Vane, thirty-one years:
  *"he's never once tried to be charming at me, which is rarer than you'd think
  in a gentleman."* He stands out of the way of barrows and thanks the lad
  pushing them, which Hesk Brun notes gentlemen don't.
- **He has offered to help and has not yet said with what.**
- **The king's own instruction stands over him:** *"Oris Caul is not dangerous
  to you. He is not dangerous to anybody… be civil to him, because he has done
  nothing wrong and he will be frightened."*

**He knows Alinar's name, rank and the date**, read off the crown ledger at the
dead counter on 22 January, and had a night with it before the knock.

**He now knows the entire contents of the mountain**, because Alinar told him,
in confidence, leaving no detail out.

---

## Mechanics

**No statblock, and no reason to build one.** Commoner-tier. He is eighty-plus in
the way elves are old, carries nothing, and has never been nearer a fight than a
college corridor.

---

## ⚠ Hard Limits

- **He does not know what is at the centre**, what the quiet ones are, or what
  the collection is for. **Nobody has ever told him anything.**
- **He must not become the reader.** Faelyn still has no reader; Kuldor and Myst
  remain the only routes and both are shut until the thaw. What he is, is the
  only person alive who knows the correspondence is **two centuries old and not
  confined to the Tempest Peaks.**
- **Do not reveal who the steward was**, or who else has brought a king a page
  of that writing. Both are open.
