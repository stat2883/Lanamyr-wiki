## Decisions and Reasoning

Recording the *why* here, because the other files record only the *what*.

### Era and premise
Set at the **start of the Elf War, 387 AW**, with the party as newly joined
Faelyn militia. The players wanted to play alongside Mya Li and witness the
events of her story rather than replace them.

**Key constraint identified:** the Elf War is not a blank slate — it is the
confirmed outline of the novel *Unlit*, with fixed beats. Player agency lives
in the spaces between those beats, not in overriding them. This shaped the whole
campaign structure. See `../reference/timeline-387-392.md`.

### The Marauder question
The players intend for both PCs to volunteer as Marauders in 392 AW. Since all
ten Marauders die in confirmed lore, this was flagged early as a commitment to
a tragic ending.

**Player ruling:** if the twins survive, the outcome is **non-canon** and is not
promoted to the bible. Killing them is permitted at any stage. The DM plays it
straight and does not protect them.

### The characters
Two Wood Elf Ranger twins, both 45 (born 342 AW), built by the players with the
DM reading rules. **Sharii** is the elder, follows his brother, and took a
deliberately non-optimal array — Dex 13 on a ranged build, with Wis 18 and
Cha 18 instead. **Alinar** is the younger and the one who actually leads.

Rolled 4d6-drop-lowest with guardrails (no score below 8, total ≥76). Sharii
rolled 87 total, Alinar exactly 76. **The disparity was flagged and the players
explicitly accepted it.**

They came out complementary rather than duplicated: Alinar is the weapon
(damage, stealth, initiative), Sharii is the senses, the voice, and the only
healing.

### Sharii's *Unbidden* — the campaign's central thread
The player established that Sharii has always felt a pull toward magic and
resisted it to follow Alinar.

This was flagged as significant, because in Lanamyr the being who taps ambient
energy directly is a **Magni** — which is what Diquyk is, and there is an
unfilled slot in `Lanamyr_08_Open_Questions.txt` for a more recent, less
powerful Magni.

The player asked for Detect Magic as an innate ability. Three mechanical
versions were offered; the player invented a better one: **involuntary,
uncontrollable, DM-triggered, and unwelcome.** Sharii cannot invoke or suppress
it. The player never rolls for it.

**Whether Sharii is a Magni is deliberately unresolved and must not be
confirmed or denied.** Multiclassing was turned OFF partly to keep this out of
the player's hands — his development in this direction is DM-granted.

Alinar knows about it and does not understand it.

Sharii also took **dragons** as Favored Enemy, unexplained. Noted as *not*
ancestral: Wood Elves are Drasu-descended, dragons are Draak-descended, so
heredity is ruled out. Source unknown and intentionally so.

### Why they enlisted
Grew up in Caldalus. Watched messengers and refugees arrive as the Stone Giant
attacks began. **Alinar wanted to lend a hand. Sharii followed him.**

Noted: this is the same impulse that drives Mya Li — compassion and a refusal to
stand by — arrived at from opposite ends of Faelyn's society.

### Table rules
DM simulates all dice. Milestone leveling at the next long rest. 2014 PHB plus
MM, DMG, Xanathar's, Tasha's. Feats on, multiclassing off. Tasha's optional
Ranger features **declined** — Natural Explorer (Forest) is too useful in a
campaign fought entirely in Faelyn's woods, and Favored Foe's concentration
cost conflicts with Alinar's spells. Track gold and key items only.

**Resurrection** exists but is exceedingly rare and costly — deliberately, so
that Kharis's death and the Marauders' sacrifice retain their weight.

### The militia
Built from scratch (bible gives no organizational detail). Deliberately small,
underfunded, and untested — Faelyn has not fought a war in living memory. Six
named squadmates plus Captain Vaeroth. **Mya Li and Kharis Ailwin are placed in
the same squadron as the PCs**, so the party witnesses her arc firsthand rather
than secondhand.

**Tam Ellisan is deliberately positioned as the first meaningful loss.** The
players were told this in advance.
