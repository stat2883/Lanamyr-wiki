# Session 007 — The Correspondence

**In-world date:** 387 AW, 3rd Epoch. Deep winter. **Days fifty through one
hundred and three.** The longest span of in-world time the campaign has covered.
**Party level:** 4 → **5** (milestone, applied at the long rest in Ashwell, day 84).
**Location:** Caldalus → Ashwell → the Tempest shoulder → vent four → vent three →
Ashwell → Caldalus → **Ashwell (forward posting)** → the vent line ×2 → the seam,
the junction, the ledge, **the staircase** → vent nine → **the ledge**.

---

## Summary

**No combat. No damage worth the name. No deaths. Seven sessions, six without an
attack roll.** The only hit points lost all session were two, to a rock.

This is the session in which the campaign changed shape. Six things:

1. **THE WARD.** *Detect Magic*, cast on a cut shelf two hundred feet down inside
   the void, returned **ABJURATION** off the cube — the largest aura Ignis has
   ever seen — **passing through** the cube and running north, down and along the
   wall past the edge of her radius. **The lamp is faint Evocation and nothing
   more. The circle has no aura at all.**
2. **THE VENTS ARE A CIRCUIT, AND THE DRAUGHT IS AN INSTRUMENT.** Some blow out,
   some draw in. **Every hole with tools under it blows out; every silent hole
   draws in. Twelve for twelve across two turns, including two reversals.**
3. **THE WINTER POSTING.** Vaeroth put the north above his own head, refused to
   write another order for the inside of that mountain, and wrote **a rota** —
   ten-day turns, entirely surface, expiring when the draw shuts.
4. **THE STAIRCASE IS UNWORN**, and goes past the lamps, and nobody has ever seen
   the bottom of that place. The lamps are not on a floor. They are on a wall.
5. **THE POST SYSTEM.** Three hand-cut recesses, at hand height, **cut to the
   size of a wax writing board** — the shaft head under vent four, the head of
   the stair, and (new) vent nine. They have built letterboxes.
6. **THE FORM REPLY.** Two blank boards, two places. The ledge board came back
   with **nine lines character-for-character identical to the original**, and
   **no answering stroke in the corner.** The vent-nine board — handed over in
   person and answered on the spot in front of a witness — came back with
   **eleven**, with **two corrections**, and **the stroke**.

> **The nine lines this company concealed from the crown are a form.**
> **The eleven lines are the only thing these people have ever actually said.**

## Scenes Played

### Days 50–55 — Caldalus, the waiting week

- The party elected to **wait to be ordered** rather than propose anything. The
  folder sat on Vaeroth's desk with the top line empty for five days. **He did
  not send for them**, deliberately, and said so later.
- **Sharii and Knight, four days of training.** He stopped trying to fix the
  whistle and diagnosed it instead: *"the whistle is an order to abandon a job he
  gave himself."* Built a **second signal** — a double tap on his own thigh,
  twice, meaning *be between me and it from over here* — which lives **inside**
  the job instead of across it. Two-thirds reliable at thirty feet, nothing at
  forty, nothing on a moving target. **Animal Handling 17.**
- **Ignis, the daily reading.** Days 50–54: **two feet, two feet, two feet, two
  feet, two feet.** A flat line. Maevis: *"That's data, and you'll not like it.
  Mind — you've no baseline."*
- **Bellis Ordow broke the deadlock** without being asked, on day 54:
  *"That order's been sat in his folder five days with nothing in the top line.
  He's not going to fill it in... the only reason the eastern one got decided is
  a lad put his hand up in a mess hall."*

### Day 55 — the office, and the objective

### Days 58–64 — the yard

- **In wolf form Knight comes all the way in.** Nose to nose, sleeps against her,
  brings her the stick instead of putting it halfway. **The moment she reverts he
  is at two feet again.**
- **Why (Persuasion 21):** as a wolf she is *one* thing — a wolf that smells of a
  place, which he has a shelf for. As herself she is **two things at once** and he
  has nowhere to put it. ***"It is not more frightening. It is less of a shape."***
- **The under-smell does not change when her body does.** Not on her clothes, not
  on her skin.
- **Told she is going back under, he asked to come**, was told no, and **merged it
  with his existing job**: *"I will be at the mark at the hour every day the whole
  time you are under."*
- **Day 63: he asked her the question again**, unprompted, with the new fact
  attached: *"You go to the underneath and you come back... He goes somewhere
  without moving. Is that the same place? Is he going under? Does he come back?"*

### Day 63–64 — ⚠ THE QUESTION IS PASSED

**Ignis told Sharii**, on the walk uphill, in full, including that she had held it
five weeks and had no account of why she hadn't. **The oldest open thread in the
campaign, closed by her, voluntarily, to his back.**

**Sharii's response was a diagnostic question:** *do I smell of it too — and if not,
what made him put us in the same question?*

**Animal Handling 23. The answer, in two halves:**

1. **"No. He has no under on him. Not ever. Not once."** Whatever is on Ignis is
   not on Sharii and never has been.
2. **"It is not a smell. It is that there is not one."** When it happens, Knight
   **cannot smell Sharii.** Everything else in the world is still there. *"There is
   a place where he is standing and there is nothing in it."* **Eleven times since
   he has known him** — a number he is certain of and cannot explain.
   *"I do not think he is going under. I do not know where he goes. **There is
   nothing there to know.** That is the whole of why I asked."*
   And: *"It does not hurt me. I would like to be told before it happens so I can
   be where you are. I know you cannot tell me. I would like it anyway."*

**Nobody said anything. Four minutes of spell left; nobody used them.**

### Day 69 — INSIDE VENT FOUR

**Wild Shape 1 of 2. Athletics 16** through the pinch — fur off both flanks.

**The passage:** cut immediately past the natural rock, **twenty inches high**, no
niches at first, **floor polished in a ten-inch band and both walls rubbed smooth
at eight inches up, continuously.** *Something comes through here tight, regularly,
for long enough to polish rock.* **Nothing six foot four walks out of vent four.**

**Two niches in the crawl. Both empty, both swept.**

**The crawl ends in an opening near the ceiling of a room, eight feet above its
floor.** *(Vent four is a hole in a roof.)*

**The room:** eighteen feet, domed. **A cube. A lamp. A closed, finished circle.**
No tools, no chips. **A shaft in the far floor with worn holds.**

**The cube: CON save 18 — SUCCESS.** No lock, no flicker, nobody climbed. And
because she was not seized she could listen:
- **Weaker here.** The same rope, further out along it.
- **The absence is adjacent.** Right there, at the edge.
- *Set beside vent nine: the thing that came up that ladder came because the cube
  did something, not because anyone was watching.*

**At the shaft head, a niche at four feet — and THE WAX TABLET IS IN IT.** Stylus
back in its spine, clean. **Nine lines, edge to edge, margins respected.** And in
the corner, beside the party's test-stroke, **a second stroke, identical.**

**She studied it and did not touch it** (Intelligence 15): nine lines, ninth short,
straight and evenly spaced with margins, **hand sized for a reader and not the
cube's hand**, characters and sequences repeat, strokes even and confident with no
false starts. **She did not turn it over and therefore cannot say what is on the
back.**

**Down the shaft (Athletics 11, bad).** The side passage is **walkable upright**
with an eighteen-inch worn band. **One of the two near rhythms stopped; the other
did not.** One came — forty feet, empty hands, no veil — **looked at her and held
it**, **showed no reaction at all to the river stone**, and then **turned side-on
and held its arm out along the passage.** *In.*

> ⚠ **Paragraph three covers being told to leave. There is nothing anywhere about
> being asked to come.**

**She went.** Two hundred feet. A lamp standing in the corridor over nothing. Past
it, **a thirty-foot chamber with no light in it at all.**

**A cube — body-warm, drumhead-soft, identical — and ABSOLUTELY SILENT.** No save,
nothing to save against. **A finished circle round it. A second, larger circle
being cut outside the first, half done, by one of them kneeling in the pitch dark.**

**Standing inside the absence, she could feel the pulse all around it in the floor
and walls, and the cube not in it.** And: **the absence does not stop at that
chamber. It runs on, north and down. More than one.** And **the work is on the
boundary, cutting from the lit side outward.**

**She offered her hand and said *if you can understand me, take my hand.*** Nothing
reacted to the sound. **It did not take her hand.** It took her **wrist**, turned
her palm up, and **drew a single short stroke across it with one finger.** Then
looked at her, looked at the passage, looked back. **She left inside a second.**

### Day 71 — Ashwell, and Aldric

Thale found the shape of the second breach, which is **not a breach at all**:
*"Last time you broke an order. This time there wasn't one. You weren't breaching
it — you were **outside** it."*

**Then the thing he came into the room for:** in the autumn Aldric asked whether
there was anything coming down off that hill he wanted his people somewhere else
for, and was told **no**. *"Is that still true?"*

**The party ruled that Aldric should be told.** Alinar found the precise shape:
*"He didn't decline it in a vacuum. He asked a question and we answered it. **The
answer's changed.** He's owed that and nothing more."*

**Aldric was told the answer is now *we don't know*, and nothing else.** He asked
three farming questions — ***When? Can we plant? Is it coming down?*** *(the wolf's
question, word for word, from a man who has never met a wolf)* — and gave three
rulings:

1. **He tells the village.** *"Sitting on a thing is how a headman turns into a liar."*
2. **Nobody goes up the draw. Including in the spring.**
3. **⚠ If it ever came to moving** — forty-one people, one neighbour in the world
   who'd take them — *"there's exactly one place, and the only reason he'd listen
   is a piece of paper your lad spent a fortnight on about **grain**. I'd like it
   written so it'd work for something else."*

> **The levy petition, the surveyed boundary and the Dorn's Ford charter stopped
> being a food arrangement in that kitchen. They are now Ashwell's only
> evacuation plan, and they lapse at the spring assessment in March.**

### Day 75–76 — Caldalus: the captain's answer

**Four questions and not a fifth**, the last of which was: ***"If I write an order
that says no — will it work?"*** Answer: **no.**

> *"Twice now, something down there has told her to leave and she has gone inside a
> second. **And nothing I have ever written has stopped her once.** What I am
> looking at is a soldier who obeys instructions issued by something I cannot
> identify, have never seen and cannot speak to. **And it has been giving her good
> ones.** That is the most frightening sentence I have ever said in this room."*

**No charge, no reprimand, and no explanation of why — because he spent that one
last session and would not spend it twice.**

**He refused to write another order for that mountain** (*"a promise that doesn't
bind anybody is theatre"*), **put the north above his own head in writing with the
tablet named in it**, and told Ignis to her face: *"There is nothing left between
you and that hole that I know how to write."*

**On Ashwell he moved inside five minutes.** The charter goes to the assessment
**as grain**, with **why** in no letter — *"the moment I write *they may have to
move*, it becomes a thing a clerk can refuse."* He credited Alinar's fortnight at a
desk as *"the only piece of this winter that is going to save anybody."*

**On the tablet he laid out three roads** — send it and lose it; keep it and be a
commander concealing a find in wartime; or **ask the king for a man who can read
it**, which costs Mya the one thing she has refused to spend all winter.

**The party chose to keep it. He refused to let them carry the blame:**

> *"I will not conceal it. **I will delay it, with a date on it, in writing, in my
> own hand.** And I will not write down where it is. The decision goes in my hand.
> **The location goes nowhere.**"*

**His date is the spring assessment. March.**

### Day 76 — the rota

**Alinar asked for open-ended orders or unwritten guidance.** Vaeroth refused both
(*"unwritten guidance is a thing a man gives so he can disown it later"*) and then
named the thing the party did not have a word for:

> *"An order that never ends is a fallback. **An order that recurs — a named
> observation, on a stated interval, with an expiry on each turn — is a rota.**"*

> **COMPANY ORDER — THE VENT LINE SURVEY**
> **1.** Walk vents one to eleven, west to east, recording **spall age, draught
> direction and strength, a timed tool count with the counter named, moss and
> bare ground, mouth dimension from outside, and anything on the snow.**
> **2. ENTIRELY SURFACE.** Ten paces and look. **Nothing taken and nothing left.**
> **3.** A turn every ten days, each with its own expiry.
> **4.** Weather beats the order, and a turn given up to weather is recorded with
> the name of whoever gave it up.
> **5.** A report goes south on a fixed day **whether or not there is anything in
> it.** *(Because silence would otherwise have two meanings.)*
> **6.** Command forward is **Sgt. S. Thale.**
> **7. Expires when the draw shuts.** Everybody comes home.
> **8. Nothing in this order authorises anything inside the ground. There is no
> order for that and there is not going to be one.**

**The costs he named himself:** it puts them four days from the only man who can
say no, and when he says no it doesn't work; six mouths in a village that nearly
ate its seed corn; Maevis is a civilian and must be asked; **and he will find out
about anything that happens inside nine days late.**

**⚠ The arithmetic he would not hide:** *the draw shuts in January; the stair
finishes in spring.* **The only stretch of the year nobody can observe is exactly
the stretch in which the job completes.** *"I'd like it on the record that I looked
for an answer and didn't find one."*

### Day 76 — the supply line, and Ordow's problem

**Goodberry solves the posting** — one 1st-level slot a night, ten berries, six

> *"I said I'd know the exact day one of you stopped drawing. **You've just told me

> *"Then you draw anyway. All six, every turn, whether you eat it or not... It's

**The party donated the drawn stores to Ashwell.** Ordow entered it as a lawful
**disbursement against a militia post billeted on their ground**, not a gift.

### Day 76 — the decoy, refused

**The party proposed a doctored copy of the tablet as a decoy. Maevis refused
flat**, with three reasons: she is not good enough at the script to forge it and a
forgery that fails tells the taker a real one exists; **a deliberate falsehood
poisons the whole corpus** (*"you don't poison one record, you poison the shelf"*);
and it would **turn the captain's documented delay into the cover story for a
fraud.**

**She offered two honest alternatives instead** and both were taken: **her existing
copy, labelled across the top** — *COPY. Taken from the original by M. Tarn, by
candlelight, in haste. I would not swear to a single character* — and **a fresh
board bearing nothing but the two matched strokes**, which is a true statement of
the entire readable content of the exchange.

**Mya:** *"The one worth protecting is the one that goes into somebody's kit and
never comes out of it and never gets mentioned in a room again. Including this one."*

**Twelve wax tablets drawn from stores**, entered in Ordow's book as a survey party
drawing writing boards. **Mya found the conflict**: the rota says *nothing is
left*. **Vaeroth agreed and would not amend it** — *"one objective per order"* — and
offered an exchange order as **its own sheet, with its own date, surface only**,
provided one thing first:

> ***"Tell me what you intend to say."***
> *"You cannot read one mark of what they sent you, and they have no way of
> knowing that, and **the most likely thing in the world is that they are waiting
> for an answer to a question we never received.**"*

### Days 96 — THE SEAM, THE JUNCTION, THE STAIRCASE

**Unbroken snow in front of the seam. Sixty-five days. They still do not use the
door this militia opened.**

**Ignis went down the corridor alone**; the party held the junction **by choice,
with no order and no count.**

- **The cut face has moved forty or fifty feet.** Finished, dressed, domed, with

- **The ledge is empty. The nine went down with the work.**
- **⚠ THE STAIRCASE IS UNWORN.** Two hundred feet of finished flights, four or five
  abreast, no wear anywhere — in a place where every single thing they use shows
  it. ***It is for somebody who has not arrived.***
- **⚠ THE LAMPS ARE NOT ON A FLOOR.** They are on cut shelves on a wall, at one
  height, and **the stair goes past them and keeps going down.**
  **Nobody has ever seen the bottom of that place.**
- **Below the lamp line it gets warmer** and **the pulse is perceptible through
  boots and wall without touching anything.**
- **The finished stair ends**, then roughed-out rock, then raw. **The frontier
  again.**
- **Nine of them on the working face, in total darkness — and they did not react
  to her arrival at all.** *(Contrast: in the autumn all nine stopped for three
  seconds when an engineer slipped eighty feet away.)*

**She held board No. 4 up in front of the nearest.** It stopped mid-stroke; **the
other eight did not.** It looked at her, looked at the board, **put one fingertip
on the stroke in the corner**, did not take it, and **looked up the stair, and
back, and up again.** *Not here. Up there. We made you somewhere.*

**⚠ THE NICHE AT THE HEAD OF THE STAIR.** Hand-cut, swept, at hand height, **cut to
the size of a wax board**, **not there in the autumn**, **empty for three months.**

**She climbed back and seated board No. 4 in it**, stylus in the spine. **It fits
exactly.** Gone one hour and ten minutes with nobody counting.

> **Maevis:** *"Your control's gone and I'd rather you heard it from me... **And I'd
> have broken it too.** They cut a box, at hand height, to size, facing the way we
> come in, and then they left it empty for a season. **That's not a people being
> approached. That's a people waiting.**"*

### Day 100 — THE GALLERY

Sixty or seventy feet down, a **cut gallery** longer than sixty feet of darkvision
in both directions.

**Twenty-four of them at least** — she counted to twenty-four and stopped because
that was where her sight ended and there were more on both sides. **In total
darkness, in silence, none looking at another.**

**They are cutting shelves.** Level, square, at a fixed interval, the whole length.
Some finished and empty, some half-cut, raw rock at the far edge. **Chips swept
left at every working position without exception.**

> **This is where the empty shelves come from. The dark arc is not a gap. It is a
> construction site.**

**⚠ *Detect Magic* in that gallery (1st-level slot) returned NOTHING.** Not the
workers, not their tools, not the finished shelves, not the walls. **The only
magical thing within thirty feet was the caster.**
***They build the housing before there is anything to put in it.***

**She tried to guide one up.** The nearest stopped, looked at her, **looked at the
shaft, looked back, and did not move** — the stair-worker's sentence handed back.
Then **a different one, well out of sight behind her, facing the wall, set its tool
down in the row by size and climbed the shaft past her without looking at her.**

*(Same as the autumn: the one furthest away, facing entirely the wrong direction,
put down its basket and walked. **Nobody spoke, nobody looked, the right one went.**)*

### Day 100 — WATCHING ONE OF THEM WRITE

She followed it up (**Athletics 15**) and found it **sitting cross-legged on the
floor of the finished room with the board on its knees and the stylus in its right
hand**, writing.

**Four minutes. Left to right, top to bottom, margins respected, nothing ruled.
Stylus held like a tool. Eyes on the middle distance, not on the wax.**

> **⚠ THE EXPERIMENT ANSWERED ITSELF BY EYE.**
> **Fluent and unbroken for the first several lines.** Then **stopped for twenty to
> thirty seconds.** Then **slower, shorter runs, and TWO CORRECTIONS** — wax
> smoothed back with the flat of the stylus and re-cut.
> ***A form does not get corrected.*** **Eleven lines. The original was nine.**

**With *Detect Magic* running the whole time, none of it was magical** — not the
stylus, not the wax, not the act — **and the cube three feet away never so much as
flickered.** *(Third independent confirmation that the writing is not the engine.)*

**It seated the stylus, stood, and put the board back in the niche** — square,
flush. **It did not hand it to her.** Then it looked at her, **looked at the niche,
looked back**, and went down the ladder.

### Day 100 — the comparison on the shelf

**Athletics 11** out through the pinch with two boards.

**For the first time in the campaign, Maevis compared two ORIGINALS rather than two
bad copies**, which is the only reason she would say any of it:

- **The first six lines are identical.** Same marks, same order, same line breaks.
  *"I would put my name on that."*
- **Then they diverge.** Original: three more, ending short. New: five more, ending
  short. **Both end on a short line.**
- **Both corrections are in the body. Neither is in the form.**

> ***"Six lines of form. A body. And a close. That is the shape of a letter, and I
> cannot read one mark of it, and I have just described its structure off two
> samples. That is actually how it is done."***

**Mya:** *"The first time, six of us put a blank board on a floor and walked away,
and they wrote three lines. This time she carried it down a ladder, held it up in
front of a man at a working face, put it in a box they cut for it, and stood there
while he answered. And he wrote five, and had to think about two of them.
**They said more because something was different. And the different thing was her.**"*

### Days 100–101 — vents ten and eleven

Ten unchanged. **Eleven: third reading, third fresh heap, strongest draught —
and MEASURABLY WARMER than vent nine**, by the back of Maevis's hand at both.
**Twenty-one minutes of listening: nothing.** Mouth **about twenty inches** —
**the second widest on the line. Somebody could go in that.**

### Days 101–103 — the road west, and the council

**Sharii asked Mya whether they know we can't understand them.** Her answer, after
two hundred yards:

> *"**Every single thing that has ever actually worked between us and them has been
> done with hands.**" (the stone on the floor; the water where a blind woman's hand
> came down; the open palm; the hand turned over and stopped four inches short; the
> look at the way out; the fingertip on the stroke.) "**Not one of those was
> writing. Every one of them worked.** And they have written us twenty lines and not
> one word has ever landed. A people who didn't know we were deaf would keep
> shouting. **These ones put the shout on a board and then take you by the hand for
> the part that matters.** They know."*
>
> ***"Which leaves a worse question. If they know we can't read it — why are they
> writing it? Somebody is meant to read that. And I don't think it's us."***
>
> **Wick:** *"...Or it's not for anybody. It's a record."*
> **Maevis:** *"Both of those go in the right-hand column and neither gets a line
> through it."*

**Ignis proposed testing the stroke:** was it copied because it is **a mark**,
because it is **Mya's**, or because **it is a tradesman's test-stroke and they
recognised the trade?**

**Maevis:** *"That is the best question anybody has asked about these people since
the autumn, and I include every single one of mine."* Then she broke the
five-marks-one-board version (*five variables, no readable result*) and gave it
back bigger: **twelve boards, a ten-day rota, one variable per board. Three turns
and you would know which of the three readings is right — thirty days, and the draw
shuts in January.**

### Day 103 — THE LEDGE, AND THE FORM REPLY

**Board No. 4 was in the niche. It is not blank.**

**Nine lines — character for character identical to the original**, same order,
same line breaks, same short ninth line. Maevis checked it twice with a straight
edge.

> ***"The place doesn't matter. You put a blank board with a stroke on it in front
> of these people and you get these nine lines. It's a form. It's a receipt."***
>
> *"We have spent thirty-three days treating that board as the only document of its
> kind in the world. The captain has a note in his own hand saying he's sitting on
> it until March. Your sergeant split it from its copy so it couldn't be taken in
> one go. **And it's a form reply.**"*

**⚠ AND THE CORNER.** Nobody had checked.

- **Original (junction, fetched within the half hour): has the answering stroke.**
- **Board No. 7 (handed over in person, answered in front of a witness): has it.**
- **Board No. 4 (left in a box, nobody met): DOES NOT.**

> *"So that stroke isn't part of the letter. **It's what passes between two people
> who have actually met.** It was on her palm. A finger, on skin, once."*
>
> **And then she refused to make it tidy:** *"**Except the original has it and
> nobody met anybody.** Six of you put that board on a floor and were a mile off
> before anything came for it. I cannot reconcile that and I am not going to.
> **Both halves go on the paper.**"*

**Mya**, holding the eleven-line board: *"Then this is the only thing they have ever
actually said to us. Three lines different. Two of them fixed twice. And half a
minute of a man on a stone floor deciding what to put."*
